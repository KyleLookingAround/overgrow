Overgrow Art Styles

The same garden drawn four ways, each with the gardener at work, three hens, slugs at the wet bed, and the same scene zoomed out as twelve allotment plots. Everything here is drawn in code, the way the game would draw it. Pick one and save; a note helps if it's "this, but…".

A

Flat top-down, soft

The garden
Zoomed out: the allotment

Rounded shapes, a natural palette, no outlines, soft shadows. Reads at any zoom because nothing depends on pixel detail; the cheapest to draw and to extend to a globe. Characters are simple and readable, not detailed.

Cost: lowest. Every level's map is the same kind of drawing.

B

Pixel art

The garden
Zoomed out: the allotment

Hard pixels on a fixed grid, a limited palette, chunky characters with personality. Charming at the garden and the allotment; at the nation and the planet the grid fights the zoom (a lorry is one pixel or the map is huge), so the top levels would use it as icons on a plainer map.

Cost: medium; sprite sheets to draw per level, and the zoom needs care to keep pixels whole.

C

Isometric

The garden
Zoomed out: the allotment

A 2:1 diamond grid with height: sheds, walls, water butts and lorries read as objects, and a busy depot looks busy. Every level's map has to be laid out on the grid and drawn back to front, and a globe doesn't fit it, so the planet would be a different projection.

Cost: highest; the drawing order and the layout rules touch every level.

D

Ink and wash

The garden
Zoomed out: the allotment

Wobbly ink lines over watercolour fills on paper, like a gardener's notebook or a field guide. Fits the educational side (the Explain card reads like a page from the same book) and scales well: a country is a wash with an ink border. Movement needs care so the line doesn't shimmer.

Cost: medium; the line and wash effects cost some drawing time, which the WebGL renderer absorbs.
