# ADR-2026-10-01: Land is organic parcels drawn by code, not hex cells

## Status

Accepted when the PR that adds it merges. It records the owner's decision of 1 Oct 2026 and supersedes decision 5 of `docs/decisions/ADR-2026-09-30-one-map.md` (land as hex cells); the rest of that record stands.

## Context

The one map's record made land that was never laid out on paper a grid of hex cells, with the art generated by code to look organic, because hexes give even neighbours, area as land and one address scheme up to the globe (H3). Asked how to start building that land, the owner chose organic parcels instead: "no grid at all", fields of any irregular shape, like real English field boundaries, split and merged along their own edges. They had moved between organic shapes and hexes while sketching on 30 Sep, and settled on organic.

## Options Considered

### Option 1: hex cells, drawn to look organic (the 30 Sep record)
**Pros:** even neighbours for everything that spreads; a cell's area is land; H3 addresses all the way to the globe. **Cons:** a regular lattice underneath every field shape, which the art has to work to hide; not what the owner wants.

### Option 2: organic parcels, fields as freely drawn polygons cut and joined geometrically
**Pros:** any shape at all. **Cons:** cutting and joining arbitrary polygons is fragile code (slivers, holes, self-touching outlines), and area and neighbours have to be recomputed after every change.

### Option 3: organic parcels built from irregular cells
The land is a seeded mosaic of irregular cells: a relaxed Voronoi diagram, no two alike, each about a third of a hectare on the smallholding. A field is any connected set of them, so joining two fields joins their sets and splitting one into strips shares its cells out, and every boundary runs along the cells' own wandering edges.
**Pros:** organic shapes at every scale with no lattice to hide; joins and splits are set operations, so area and land are conserved exactly; neighbours come free from shared edges. **Cons:** a strip's edge wanders with the cells rather than running ruler-straight (as old strips often did); no H3 for the globe.

## Decision

Option 3 (the owner, 1 Oct 2026):

1. **Land that was never laid out on paper is organic parcels**, from the smallholding's fields out: a seeded mosaic of irregular cells, a field any connected set of them, next to fields of any kind. Beds, plots and planned streets stay straight.
2. **The cells are never drawn.** The art (outlines traced and smoothed, hedges, furrows at each field's angle, woods, water, stock) is generated from the fields' outlines and cached until they change or a season turns.
3. **Land that remembers** works on cells: consolidation joins neighbouring fields up to about eighteen cells; inheritance splits a field into strips along its cells. Area is conserved by construction.
4. **The globe** is answered with level 8: the nation and the planet are drawn from their places' totals, and whether their land needs cells at all is that part's decision.

## Consequences

- The one map's spec says organic parcels where it said hex cells, and its build order's second step is "Land built from organic parcels".
- The model is our own code (a seeded, relaxed Voronoi diagram over the land's boundary), with no new dependency.
- Things that spread (pests, water, fire) find their neighbours from shared cell edges; a cell has five to seven, not exactly six.
- The land is built ahead of the levels that use it (`docs/briefs/organic-land.md`), and part 11 wires it to the smallholding.
