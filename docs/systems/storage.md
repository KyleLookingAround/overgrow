# Storage and spoilage

How long produce keeps, where it is and how warm, what is lost at each stage from field to plate, what a cold store costs and saves, and what a van's round burns. Written ahead of part 14 (the first market and the road) in `src/sim/models/storage.ts`, with its plausibility test `src/sim/models/storage.test.ts` and its numbers in `src/data/storage.ts`. It imports the household model (`footprintOf`, for the land and carbon behind wasted food) and the energy model (`coldStoreKWh`, prices, carbon, `burn`'s loads and the weather's mean temperature). Nothing calls it yet: the `storage` system is not in `src/sim/systems.ts`, so it changes no pacing until part 14 wires it. The market notes are `docs/systems/market.md`.

- **Shelf life and Q10** (`shelfLife`, `rateFactor`, `keepsIn`): each product has days it keeps at 20 °C (`shelf`) and a Q10 (2 to 3.5: spoilage speeds up that many times for every 10 °C, Labuza; Tijskens and Polderdijk). A dark, ventilated store (a shed or a cold store) keeps potatoes 2.5 times longer (`dark`: less greening and sprouting). Salad keeps about 3 days in a 15 °C shed and about 11 in a cold store; potatoes about 4 months in the shed and 9 in the cold. Products: the six crops, eggs, milk and fresh meat (the last three are the livestock model's, defined here by their own keys because that model hasn't merged; part 14 checks the product names match).
- **Where produce is** (`tempAt`): the field is the outside air; a shed follows it 70 % of the way toward 15 °C; a cold store is 4 °C, or the coldest the product likes (`coldMin`: tomatoes 10 °C, beans 7 °C, potatoes 5 °C, since they suffer chilling injury or turn sweet); a van is 4 °C above the outside air; a shop shelf is a room (two-fifths outside) but chilled at 8 °C for what can take 4 °C (salad, eggs, milk, meat); a home is 14 °C, the fridge and the worktop mixed.
- **Lots** (`Lot`, `age`, `survives`, `blendLife`): a lot is kg of a product and the `life` it has used (0 fresh, 1 gone). Ageing adds days over the shelf life at the place's temperature; the share of a lot left is `1 − life⁵`, so little goes early and the rest goes together. Kg are conserved: a lot's kg after plus what it lost is what it was. Survivors keep the lot's life (a mean, not a spread).
- **The home stage and the kitchen** (`homeWaste`, `TEMPS.home`): the calibration is the kitchen's own days. At 14 °C every crop's shelf life falls within 20 % of `keeps.kitchen` in `src/data/crops.ts`, and half of it (capped at `KEEP_DAYS`, 21) is what the kitchen keeps, also within 20 %. A household eats a purchase evenly over `eatDays`; what it gets to after the food has turned is wasted. Part 14 swaps the kitchen's fixed `keeps.kitchen` for `shelfLife(product, TEMPS.home)`, changing nothing at 14 °C, and its exponential going-off for `age(lot, 'home', 1, …)`.
- **Losses by stage** (`chainLosses`, `typicalChain`, `lossShares`): kg harvestable in, the unharvested and the graded out lost at the field, then the store, the van, the shelf and the home each ageing what's left. A typical chain of a household's veg (the basket's mix of potatoes, salad, tomatoes and greens, each held and shelved its usual days) at 12 °C loses about 29 % between field and plate: home about 57 % of what's lost, the farm about 39 %, the store 3 %, the shelf 1 %, the road 0.1 %. That is WRAP's order (households about 70 % of UK post-farm food waste by weight, retail about 4 %; FAO's Europe has about a fifth of fruit and veg lost at production and a fifth at consumption), so the Explain card can say where food is wasted. Warmer weather moves loss from the home to the store and shelf.
- **What waste carries** (`wasteFootprint`): the carbon growing it took plus where it went (landfill methane about 0.5 kg CO₂e a kg, compost 0.1, feed 0.05), and the land behind it in m² a year (Poore & Nemecek, through the household model), so a wasted kg of salad is about 1.1 kg CO₂e and 0.4 m² a year, and a kg of meat far more.
- **The cold store** (`coldCost`, `coldBenefit`, `roomFor`, `hold`): the energy model's kWh (about 0.6 kWh a m³ a day at 15 °C outside, 0.04 more a degree), priced at 27p and 0.2 kg CO₂e a kWh; a cubic metre holds 40 kg of salad and 250 of potatoes. `coldBenefit` compares holding a lot in a shed with holding it cold: the kg saved at a price a kg against the room's bill. 120 kg of salad for 4 days at 22 °C saves about 120 kg (£360) for £2.85 and 2 kg CO₂e; 4 tonnes of potatoes for 90 days in a 5 °C winter save about 27 kg (£30) for £78; held through a warm 12 °C autumn they save 214 kg (£235) for £187 and 138 kg CO₂e. No setting wins everywhere.
- **The van's round** (`round`): the run to the first stop and back, plus 0.7 km a stop, at 32 km/h and 3 minutes a stop, on 9 L/100 km of diesel (energy.ts's price and factor), in as many rounds as the 60-box capacity needs. Forty boxes are 36 km, 3.1 hours, 3.2 L of diesel, £2.75 and 8.7 kg CO₂e; the produce is aboard for half the round on average and sits at the outside air plus 4 °C. `machinery.ts` carries a tractor only, so the van's fuel goes through `energy.ts`'s `burn`, at the node of whatever runs the round.
- **The system** (`storage`): a `day` tick ages every node that has a `keeping` lever (`{place, life by product}`) by the step's days at the outside air's mean temperature; a `shed` with a cold store in its `loads` lever (energy.ts burns and pays that) ages as cold. What goes off leaves the node's `food.<product>` stock to `decay`, and the same kg arrive as `kgWaste` in the node's `waste` stock, as the kitchen does, for the heap. It refuses a plan on `keeping`. It draws no dice.
- **Fast effect** kg gone off today, produce dulling, a cold room's bill. **Slow effect** the share of the harvest wasted at each stage across the year, and the carbon and land behind it.
- **Speed** (Node 22, the session's 4-core 2.1 GHz Xeon, from the test's timer): a day of nine products aged, a round and a typical chain about 23 µs; the system's day tick over a store and a shelf a few µs. The store, the van and the shop's share of a smallholding's day is about 60 µs with `market.md`'s figure, far inside the parts-5-to-14 reserve.

## How it fits and grows

1. **Born where:** hands-on at the smallholding (a store node, the van, the shop's shelf, the customers' homes), moving kg of produce, kWh, £ and kg CO₂e, conserved: every kg harvested is sold, eaten, spoiled (to the heap), graded out (to compost, feed or waste) or still in store.
2. **Across the ladder:** a sealed smallholding carries up its Freshness (the life left when it reaches a customer), its losses by stage and its share of the market; at the market town it is one of many suppliers with its own store and van, and at the region the same numbers are averages over farms. The role reversal: at the garden the player is the household whose salad goes off in four days; at the smallholding the seller who decides what to store and how; at level 5 the market that sets the standards.
3. **Loops:** the waste loop (cosmetic standards and cheap food → waste → methane and lost land) and the energy loop (the cold store's power price and grid carbon). Fast: kg gone off today and a day's bill. Slow: the year's waste by stage and the carbon and land behind it.
4. **People:** a person drives the round (its hours, 3 for forty boxes), a person packs; both are `labour.ts` tasks at part 14 (`workNeeded`'s hours by role); the player delegates the round and the packing to a hired hand or the partner, and "let them decide" picks what to hold and what to sell.
5. **The lever:** the cold store on or off, what to hold and for how long (the player picks by what is stored), the round's day; nothing is set for the player.
6. **The map:** the van on the road and boxes on doorsteps; produce dulling on the shelf and going to the heap; the shop's queue is in `market.md`.
7. **Explain:** as under Wiring.
8. **Economy and balance:** kWh and £ the cold store uses (a 20 m³ room about £4 a day at 15 °C), diesel for the round (£2.75 a round of forty), and the kg lost. Wiring shifts the smallholding's first-sale and first-box milestones (not yet baselined); nothing at the garden.
9. **Carbon and land:** the cold store's electricity, the round's diesel, the methane of waste and the land behind wasted food, in `wasteFootprint`.
10. **Polish:** the store panel is a list of what is held with its life as a bar (a coloured strip, not only a number) that wraps at 320 px; a table beside the map on large screens; concise UK English ("keeps 4 days", "cold store on").
11. **The lesson:** most food waste by weight is at home and on the farm, not in shops; cold storage trades energy for less waste and pays for salad in summer and not for potatoes in winter.
12. **Unfolding:** the shelf-life clock shows with the first stored harvest (`store.shelfLife`), the cold store's bill and saving with the first cold store bought (`store.cold`), and the losses by stage with the first week of a box or shop sale that leaves produce unsold (`store.losses`): proposed `unfold.ts` keys, not added.

## Numbers and what they simplify

Shelf lives are calibrated to the kitchen's days at 14 °C, not read from a table, and the Q10s are mid-range values for fresh produce; grading shares, hold days and eating days are chosen so a typical chain's losses land in WRAP's and FAO's order and rough size. A lot has one age; a step's temperature is steady; a van and a shed are simple offsets from the outside air; humidity, ethylene and handling damage are folded into the shelf life. Eggs, milk and meat are defined for the livestock model's products by name only.
