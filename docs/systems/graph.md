# The graph

Every level is one graph of nodes and flows (`docs/decisions/ADR-2026-09-28-scale-free-graph.md`). `src/sim/graph.ts` defines it; `src/sim/state.ts` builds the back garden's from `src/data/garden.ts`; `src/sim/churn.ts` is the test-only system the conservation test moves flows with.

- **Units in the types.** A quantity is a number branded with its unit (`Qty<'L'>`, `Qty<'kgN'>`, `Qty<'kgCO2e'>`, `Qty<'kgFood'>`, `Qty<'h'>`, `Qty<'kWh'>`, `Qty<'GBP'>`, `Qty<'m2'>` and the rest of `Unit`). Make one with `qty(n, unit)`; `add()` and `sub()` refuse two units at compile time (`src/sim/graph.test.ts` proves it with `@ts-expect-error`). Food, feed and waste are kg by product: a stock or flow carries `product`.
- **A node** (`GraphNode`) has a `kind` (what draws it and what acts on it), a `box` on its level's map in metres (null for a node not drawn, like the air), `stocks` by key, `levers` (plans, policies and laws a system declares by giving the node a default) and `totals`. `makeNode()` gives every node a `carbon` stock (kg CO₂e) and at least one `land.<use>` stock (m²), and `Totals` carries the five headline numbers (Output, Quality, Reliability, Upkeep, Health), Freshness, carbon and land from the start, so sealing (part 7) fills them in without adding a field. Until then `totals.land` is the land a node started with, not kept up to date: part 7 derives the totals from the stocks.
- **Edges** (`Edge`) say which nodes are joined and which units travel between them: a hose carries litres, a path carries food. A flow between two nodes needs one.
- **Flows** (`Flow`) are the only way a stock changes. Each leaves one end and arrives at the other; an end is a node's stock or a named `Boundary` (rain, evapotranspiration, drainage, runoff, mains, bought, sold, eaten, time, grid). Emissions go to the level's `atmosphere` node, so carbon balances inside the graph and the top bar's dial reads its stock. `flowProblem()` refuses a flow with no edge, the wrong unit or product, a missing stock to leave, or an amount that isn't a positive number; `applyFlow()` moves one, and a system moves one through its tick context (`ctx.flow`), which records a refusal in the snapshot's `errors`. The graph doesn't stop a stock going below zero (money can be overdrawn); a model that can't have less than nothing (water in a butt) keeps to it itself, and to a stock's `cap`.
- **Conservation.** `stockTotals()` sums every stock by unit and product; `imbalance()` compares two sums with what crossed the boundary. `src/sim/conservation.test.ts` (the `conservation` test) checks every tick of two years of the game's own systems, and of `src/sim/churn.ts`, a test-only system that moves flows of every kind, and proves it catches a system that changes a stock without a flow.
- **The garden** (`gardenGraph()`): about 12 × 8 m behind the house, with the lawn (what's left of the land), the kitchen (the household: its `money` is the purse), the path, six beds of 2 × 1.5 m (two dug, as `land.crops`; four under grass), the shed, the tap, a 200 L butt half full, the compost heap and the air. Each bed and the lawn has its soil's stocks (`docs/systems/soil.md`), its carbon the soil's humus, and the air carries the day's weather as its `weather` lever (`docs/systems/weather.md`). Digging a bed is a flow of m² from its `land.grass` to its `land.crops`. Nothing grows yet: the crops and the gardener come in part 3.
