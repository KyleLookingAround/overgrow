# Soil

Each bed's and the lawn's soil: the water it holds, its organic matter, its nutrients and its health (`src/sim/models/soil.ts`, with its plausibility test in `src/sim/models/soil.test.ts`), from the textures and starting sizes in `src/data/soils.ts` and each place's soil in `src/data/garden.ts`; laid down by `gardenGraph()` in `src/sim/state.ts`; shown in the panel (`src/ui/Panel.tsx`).

- **The stocks** (`SOIL`), on every bed and the lawn: `water` (L in the 30 cm layer), `carbon` (humus, kg CO₂e: the node's own carbon stock), `carbon.fresh` (fresh plant matter: roots, litter, and compost the gardener spreads), `nitrogen.organic` (kg N in the organic matter), `nitrate` (kg N, free to leach), `phosphorus` (kg P, Olsen) and `potassium` (kg K). Other models move their inputs and outputs as flows between these and other nodes: compost into `carbon.fresh` and `nitrogen.organic` and digging moving `land.grass` to `land.crops` (`docs/systems/carbon.md`), crops taking `nitrate`, `phosphorus` and `potassium` (`docs/systems/crops.md`); legumes adding nitrogen comes later. There's nothing else to call.
- **Water held** (`hydraulics()`, `limitsOf()`): Saxton & Rawls (2006) from the sand, clay and organic matter give the water at wilting point, field capacity and saturation, and how fast a saturated soil drains. As organic matter falls, so does the water a soil holds. The dug beds are a loam at 4.5 % organic matter; the lawn and the plots under it a clay loam at 4 %. Every soil starts at field capacity, as British soils are in March.
- **Organic matter** (the `day` tick), RothC in spirit: humus decays at RothC's 0.02 a year and fresh matter at 1 a year, each scaled by RothC's factors for the day's mean temperature, the soil's moisture and cover (grass slows it to 0.6); of what decays, about a fifth (RothC's split by clay) becomes humus and the rest goes to the air node as CO₂. The nitrogen freed with the carbon (C:N of 10) becomes nitrate. Grass puts carbon back from the air (about 4 t C/ha a year, by degree-days above 5 °C while it has water), taking nitrate with it into the organic matter. So a bare bed loses two to three percent of its organic matter a year (most in the first, as the fresh matter goes), its carbon on the top bar's dial, while grass holds steady. At a longer step the decay runs on the step's mean temperature.
- **Nutrients.** Starting at RB209's index 2 for phosphorus and potassium and 40 kg N/ha of nitrate. Nitrate leaves with drainage in proportion to the water that drains (`leach()`), so a wet winter on bare soil loses most of the autumn's; phosphorus and potassium stay until crops take them. A rich bare bed with nothing growing is the worst case: about 2 % of its organic matter a year (Rothamsted's bare fallow in its early decades) frees and leaches about 150–300 kg N/ha a year, the most in the first, well above an arable field's; crops take some of it up (`docs/systems/crops.md`), and a cover crop would more.
- **Health** (`health()`, written to the node's `totals.health` each day): organic matter (0 at 1 %, full at 6 %), structure (`structure()`: the organic carbon to clay ratio, 0 at 1/20, full at 1/8, after Johannes et al. 2017) and moisture stress (full with half the available water or more, less as it dries and when waterlogged), weighted 4:3:3.
