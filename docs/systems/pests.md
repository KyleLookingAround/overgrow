# Pests

Slugs, aphids and potato and tomato blight in the garden, and the gardener's pest policy (`src/sim/models/pests.ts`, with its plausibility test in `src/sim/models/pests.test.ts`), from the numbers in `src/data/pests.ts`; the damage goes onto the crop through `harm()` in `src/sim/models/crops.ts`, the gardener does the policy's work (`src/sim/gardener.ts`), the plan's pest lines are in the Garden tab (`src/ui/GardenTab.tsx`), and the map draws the pests (`src/ui/map/life.ts`, and blight and nibbled leaves in `src/ui/map/draw.ts`).

- **The state.** Slugs and aphids are stocks in the `pests` unit, `pests.slugs` (product `slugs`) on each dug bed and on the lawn (its edge, where slugs shelter) and `pests.aphids` (product `aphids`) on the beds, so every one born (`growth`), arriving or leaving (`wild`), dying, eaten or caught (`decay`) is a flow the `conservation` test covers. A bed's `pests` lever holds the slugs out this hour and the most out last night, when slime was last left, the share of its tops blighted, when its pellets, insecticide and fungicide run out, when blighted leaves were last picked off, and the kg of produce lost to pests. The lawn's `outbreak` lever holds the run of Smith days, when the last Smith period completed, the garden's blight spores and when it last rained. The pests system refuses any command on either. Slugs creep between the lawn and each bed along an edge `gardenGraph()` adds (`slugs-bed-<n>`, carrying `pests`).
- **Slugs** (the `hour` tick, at the garden's hour step): after dark (between the weather's sunset and sunrise) a share of a bed's slugs is out: 60 % while it rains or for 18 hours after, 25 % on moist soil, 14 % on damp and 4 % on dry, none below 3 °C. Seedlings (a crop up but less than a fifth of the way to its first harvest) lose 0.12 % of a m²'s stand to each slug out for an hour, taken off the growth still to come; a grown crop loses 0.05 g of leaf a slug-hour, from what's ripe on it first (a `slugs` flow of food to `decay`), the rest as a little of its yield to come. Pellets, while they last, kill 8 % of the slugs out each hour. Each day (the `day` tick) slugs breed in mild (5–20 °C), moist weather, most in spring and autumn, up to 30 a m² of bed or 400 in the lawn's edge, and die faster on dry soil and after a hard frost; on a wet night 2 % of the lawn's edge crawls onto each planted bed, and slugs leave a bed with nothing growing.
- **Aphids** (daily): on the crops that draw them (beans the favourite, lettuce half as keen, tomatoes less), winged migrants arrive from 15 May to 31 July once the day's mean reaches 11 °C; the population grows by 0.018 a day for each degree of the mean above 4 °C (to 25 °C), crowding towards 3,000 a m²; they die at 3 % a day, a heavy rain (10 mm) washes off 15 %, and a crowded colony sends a tenth a day away. The garden's ladybirds (`docs/systems/biodiversity.md`) go where the aphids are and each eats up to 50 a day (Holling's type II, half that at 150 a m²). The sap they take is a share of the day's growth, half of it at 1,500 a m² or more.
- **Blight** (daily): a Smith day is a minimum of 10 °C or more with 11 humid hours or more; the weather has no humidity, so an hour counts as humid while it rains or while the air is within 2 °C of the day's minimum (the dew point by FAO-56's rule for a humid climate), a degree more on a wet day. Two Smith days in a row are a Smith period: every living potato and tomato crop that's up starts at 0.5 % of its tops blighted (more with spores left in the garden), which spreads logistically at 35 % a day in humid weather, 10 % otherwise and 3 % on a hot day, each new share of blighted tops taking the same share off the growth still to come; ripe tubers rot at 2 % a day times the infection and fruit at 10 %, into the bed's waste for the heap. When a blighted crop ends, half its infection is left in the garden as spores, which halve over a year.
- **The pest policy** (the gardener's levers `slugs`, `aphids` and `blight`, each set by a `policy` command, not a plan; `POLICIES` lists each pest's choices, and a new game picks slugs and leaves the rest, `START_POLICY`): **leave**; **pick** (slugs: a torch patrol at dusk, 10 minutes a bed, catching half the slugs out; aphids: squashing colonies over 30 a m², 10 minutes, 60 %; blight: picking off blighted leaves, 10 minutes, 30 % of the infection and a slower spread for a day); **trap** (slugs only: a home-made trap checked each morning, 4 minutes a bed, 30 % of last night's slugs; part 6's beer trap does better); **treat** (bought from the purse, skipped if it's short: ferric phosphate pellets, 3 minutes and £0.10 a bed, two weeks, with a trace of phosphorus to the soil; a pyrethrin spray on aphids over 30 a m², 5 minutes and £0.15, 90 % of them and a knock to the ladybirds and bees; a protectant fungicide on potatoes and tomatoes from June to September, 5 minutes and £0.25 a week, cutting infection and spread by 80 % until it runs out or 10 mm of rain washes it off, a little hard on ladybirds). The gardener's patrol is kept back from the day's hours first and goes out half an hour after sunset (not before 20:00) on a damp evening to each planted bed with slugs; the rest are daytime jobs (`docs/systems/gardener.md`), and each calls `control()` as it ends.
- **On the map**: slugs out after dark on each bed (up to a dozen, creeping), the slime they leave fading over a day, bites out of nibbled plants, aphid clusters on the plants, blight browning the tops, and the gardener's torch; each bed's slugs, aphids, blight and what's been lost show in the panel, and a slug, a cluster or a badge opens its Explain card (`docs/systems/explain.md`).
- **The pace** on seeds 1–3 over two years with the default plan and policy: about 15 % less picked than with no pests (50–53 kg against 59–62), most of it blight on the tomatoes and aphids on the beans with no flowers; picking, trapping or treating every pest brings it back to within a kilogram or two of no pests at all, at the gardener's time or the purse's money.
