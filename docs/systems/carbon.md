# Carbon and land

The compost heap, compost going back to the beds, and digging a bed out of the lawn (`src/sim/models/carbon.ts`, with its plausibility test in `src/sim/models/carbon.test.ts`); the gardener carries waste and compost and digs (`src/sim/gardener.ts`); the soil model takes over once compost is in a bed or a plot is dug (`src/sim/models/soil.ts`); the top bar's dial reads the air node (`src/ui/TopBar.tsx`).

- **To the heap** (`toHeap()`): green waste (residue, spoiled produce, kitchen scraps; 12 % dry matter, 42 % of it carbon, 3 % nitrogen) moves from a bed or the kitchen to the heap's `waste` stock, with the carbon its plants took from the air (from the air node to the heap's `carbon`, counted as it reaches the heap) and its nitrogen (from `growth` to the heap's `nitrogen.organic`).
- **Composting** (the `day` tick): the heap breaks down 3 a year at RothC's temperature factor of 1 (about 9 °C), faster warm, slower cold. Of each kg broken down, a quarter becomes finished compost (the heap's `compost`, product `compost`; the rest, mostly water, goes to `decay`), 45 % of its carbon goes to the air as CO₂ and the rest stays, a fifth of its nitrogen is lost to `decay`, and IPCC 2006's composting factors, scaled to green waste (about 1.2 g CH₄ and 0.09 g N₂O a kg), put about 0.06 kg CO₂e a kg into the air from `decay`. So a heap stores most of its carbon and emits the rest; while its compost sits there, the dial reads it as a small sink.
- **Spreading** (`spread()`): before sowing, the gardener spreads up to 5 kg/m² of finished compost: its mass to `decay` (it becomes soil), its carbon (about 0.4 kg CO₂e a kg) into the bed's `carbon.fresh` and its nitrogen into `nitrogen.organic`, where the soil model decays and mineralises them.
- **Digging** (`dig()`): a plot's `land.grass` moves to `land.crops`, a square metre at a time. The land-use change's soil carbon effect is the soil model's: dug, bare ground decays faster and gets none of grass's input, so over twenty years a dug plot keeps about 0.5–0.85 of the grass's soil carbon, about IPCC's 0.69 for long-term cultivation. The plan's `dig` lever on a bed asks for it (part 6's "more beds" shows it).
- **Peat** (part 6): a bag of peat compost will come in from the `bought` boundary with its carbon, its extraction's emissions from `bought` to the air, spread the same way.
