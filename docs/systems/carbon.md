# Carbon and land

The compost heap, compost going back to the beds, and digging a bed out of the lawn (`src/sim/models/carbon.ts`, with its plausibility test in `src/sim/models/carbon.test.ts`); the gardener carries waste and compost and digs (`src/sim/gardener.ts`); the soil model takes over once compost is in a bed or a plot is dug (`src/sim/models/soil.ts`); the top bar's dial reads the air node (`src/ui/TopBar.tsx`).

- **To the heap** (`toHeap()`): green waste (residue, spoiled produce, kitchen scraps; 12 % dry matter, 42 % of it carbon) moves from a bed or the kitchen to the heap's `waste` stock, with the carbon its plants took from the air (from the air node to the heap's `carbon`, counted as it reaches the heap) and its nutrients (`plant nutrients`) into the heap's `nitrogen.organic`, `phosphorus` and `potassium`: from a bed, its share of the nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium the bed's finished crops left in their residue (the bed's `waste.nitrogen`, `waste.phosphorus` and `waste.potassium`, `docs/systems/crops.md`); from anywhere else, fresh produce's (about 2.5 g N, 0.3 g P and 3 g K a kg, McCance and Widdowson; `PRODUCE` in `src/data/crops.ts`), from `growth`, where the food's nutrients went. The panel shows the heap's phosphorus and potassium beside its nitrogen, and hides a bed's `crop.*` and `waste.*` nutrients (the crop and its green waste stand for them). The bed-to-heap ways carry `kgP` and `kgK` as well as `kgN`.
- **Composting** (the `day` tick): the heap breaks down 3 a year at RothC's temperature factor of 1 (about 9 °C), faster warm, slower cold. Of each kg broken down, a quarter becomes finished compost (the heap's `compost`, product `compost`; the rest, mostly water, goes to `decay`), 45 % of its carbon goes to the air as CO₂ and the rest stays, a fifth of the nitrogen in what broke down (the heap's, by the waste it was made from) is lost to `decay`, its phosphorus and potassium all stay, and IPCC 2006's composting factors, scaled to green waste (about 1.2 g CH₄ and 0.09 g N₂O a kg), put about 0.06 kg CO₂e a kg into the air from `decay`. So a heap stores most of its carbon and emits the rest; while its compost sits there, the dial reads it as a small sink.
- **Spreading** (`spread()`): before sowing, the gardener spreads up to 5 kg/m² of finished compost: its mass to `decay` (it becomes soil), its carbon (about 0.4 kg CO₂e a kg) into the bed's `carbon.fresh`, and its share of the heap's nutrients (the heap is well mixed: a kg carries the heap's nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium over the compost there and the compost its waste will make) its nitrogen into `nitrogen.organic`, where the soil model decays and mineralises them, and its phosphorus and potassium into the bed's `phosphorus` and `potassium`. So the P and K a crop leaves in its residue come back to the beds, none made or lost on the way; what left as food doesn't, less the kitchen's scraps (Bug #47, `docs/systems/soil.md`). Compost of vegetable residue carries about 0.2 % P and 1–2 % K fresh, richer than WRAP's PAS 100 green composts (0.1–0.3 % P, 0.3–0.8 % K).
- **Digging** (`dig()`): a plot's `land.grass` moves to `land.crops`, a square metre at a time. The land-use change's soil carbon effect is the soil model's: dug, bare ground decays faster and gets none of grass's input, so over twenty years a dug plot keeps about 0.5–0.85 of the grass's soil carbon, about IPCC's 0.69 for long-term cultivation. The plan's `dig` lever on a bed asks for it (part 6's "more beds" shows it).
- **Peat** (part 6): a bag of peat compost will come in from the `bought` boundary with its carbon, its extraction's emissions from `bought` to the air, spread the same way.
- **Explain.** The carbon dial in the top bar opens the carbon card, and the heap's flows are effects at the heap (`docs/systems/explain.md`).

## The playable garden (#54)

- **Digging** (`dig()`) also pays the edging from the purse (`edging`) and sends a flush of the soil's carbon to the air, 0.03 kg CO₂e a m² (`DIG` in `src/data/garden.ts`; Reicosky & Lindstrom 1993).
- **A green manure dug in** (`digIn()`): the crop finished, then its residue's carbon (from the air, as the heap's plant carbon) goes into the bed's fresh organic matter and its nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium into the bed's pools, none of it to the heap.
- **The compost bin** (`docs/systems/shed.md`): `heapDay()` breaks down 1.5 times as fast, loses 12 % of its nitrogen instead of 20 %, and makes 1.25 times the methane and nitrous oxide while the garden owns one.
