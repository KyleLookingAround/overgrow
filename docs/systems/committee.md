# The allotment committee

Motions, votes, persuasion, political capital and the rules a passed motion sets, and burning garden waste against composting it. Written ahead of parts 8 to 10 (`src/sim/models/committee.ts`, with its plausibility test in `src/sim/models/committee.test.ts` and its numbers in `src/data/committee.ts`). It reads the agents and relationships of `docs/systems/agency.md`. Part 8 wired its first vote (`docs/systems/season.md`): the `committee` node and system at level 2, and the water rota put by a neighbour in the first dry spell. The rest waits for part 10.

- **Motions** (`MOTIONS`): the water rota (fixed slots against first come first served), water by need (part 8's: the trough shared by what each plot needs), the bonfire ban (the waste system's burning against composting: Q11, the seed of the stubble-burning ban), a plot let go to the bees, and the hosepipe rule (Q8 b: cans only from the trough until the water company lifts its ban). Each costs its proposer political capital (2 to 4 points) and has an `appeal` to each want, from −1 to 1, and a nudge for the habits it sits well or badly with (tidy and lazy neighbours resist the bonfire ban).
- **A vote** (`lean`, `hold`, `voteOf`): a member's lean is their goals' weights times the motion's appeal, plus the habit's nudge, a dry summer's push (`dryness`, mostly the hosepipe rule's), and their goodwill towards the proposer, then a day's mood from the `Rng`. Above 0.02 they vote yes, below −0.02 no, between abstain. The player's vote counts as one; a motion passes when more vote yes than no and a tie leaves things as they are (the veto-player rule: the status quo has an advantage). On a random allotment the rota passes in about half of seeds, the bees' plot in a little under half, the bonfire ban in about a quarter, and a hosepipe rule in nearly none in a wet year and all in a very dry one.
- **Persuasion** (`persuasion`): each hour spent talking to a member moves their lean towards your vote by up to 0.25 in all, with diminishing returns (`1 − e^(−h/2)`), more for someone who likes you (`× (0.5 + goodwill)`). It shifts a close vote and not a lopsided one, because a lean set by goals and goodwill is a bigger number than any talk can move.
- **Political capital** (`propose`, `canPropose`, `capitalAfter`): a stock from 0 to 8, starting at 4, spent on proposing (win or lose), refilled each week by 0.4 at neutral goodwill, doubled at full goodwill and nothing at none. A season at neutral goodwill buys roughly one more motion: the spec's "levers that cost political capital, replenished by support".
- **After the vote** (`aftermath`): voting the way a member did earns 1 % of their goodwill, voting against them costs 3 %, and members who lost to a motion you proposed are 2 % sour. So voting with a majority you didn't want keeps goodwill and the motion carries anyway; voting your preference, where your vote decides, wins you the outcome and costs goodwill with the side you beat. A motion put by another member (`put`, the water company's hosepipe rule) costs no capital.
- **The rules and what they cost** (`Rules`, `applyMotion`, `effects`): the rota is open (about a quarter of an hour queueing in a wet week, rising to an hour and three quarters at the driest) or in slots (a flat half hour), so each wins in different weather; a hosepipe rule halves the trough's litres a plot (300 a day) until `lifted`; a bonfire ban ends the half of garden waste that was burnt; each bees' plot costs a plot (up to three, and never yours or a neighbour's) and adds 2 % pollination for the rest.
- **Burning against composting** (`wasteCarbon`): a kg of green waste holds 0.66 kg of CO₂ the plants took from the air. Burnt, 95 % is back in the air at once with open burning's methane and nitrous oxide, about 0.85 kg CO₂e a kg; composted, about 0.19 kg CO₂e of methane and nitrous oxide goes at once, 0.4 kg of CO₂ over a year, and 0.26 kg of carbon is kept in the compost, against 0.03 as ash and char. IPCC 2006 defaults. Over the year burning still puts more in the air, and the heap is the only route that keeps carbon. The waste system owns the vote's consequence; this is the seed.
- **The system and the graph** (`committee`, `committeeOf`, `writeCommittee`, `proposeOn`): a `week` tick refills capital from the mean goodwill of the neighbours' relationships and shows that goodwill in a stock. `proposeOn(graph, motion, hold, rng)` is the pure half of the propose command: it builds the committee from the graph, votes and writes capital, rules and relationships back, returning the tally or why it can't. The system refuses a plan on `rules`: they're voted on.
- **Speed** (Node 22, after a warm-up, from the test's timer): a week and a vote for eleven members and the player about 15–30 µs.

## How it fits and grows

1. **Born where.** Hands-on at the allotment (level 2): the committee a node whose stocks are goodwill and political capital; flows are hours spent talking and, through the rules, water and waste.
2. **Across the ladder.** A sealed allotment carries its goodwill (a Health part) and its rules. One level up the parish and the boards reuse `hold`, `persuasion` and capital, with motions that have appeals to the same wants; you vote at 2 and serve whoever is elected at 7 (the spine).
3. **Loops.** The politics loop (rules are made by people with their own goals, capital comes from support) and, through Q14, the carbon loop.
4. **People.** The eleven neighbours as members, each with goals, habit and goodwill; the water company as a proposer.
5. **The lever.** Propose, talk to members, vote, and hand the vote to the majority.
6. **The map.** The committee meeting at the shed; slots on the trough; the bees' plot in flower.
7. **Explain.** How a vote turned: each member's lean by cause (goals, habit, goodwill, dryness, persuasion).
8. **Economy and balance.** The hours persuasion costs (a few a motion) and what each rule saves or costs in queue hours, litres and burning; wiring will shift the bot's hours at the allotment.
9. **Carbon and land.** Bonfires against compost (Q11); a plot to the bees is land let go for pollination.
10. **Polish.** At 320 px the motion is a card with its cost and two buttons, the room a row of hands; concise UK English.
11. **The lesson.** Rules are made by people with their own goals, and a majority you vote with is goodwill you keep.
12. **Unfolding.** The committee's panel with the first motion, as the proposed keys above.
