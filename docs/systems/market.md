# The box scheme and the farm shop

The smallholding's first market beyond the gate: a box scheme of subscriber households, a farm shop with walk-in customers, cosmetic grading, seasonal prices and the imports that fill a short box. Written ahead of part 14 in `src/sim/models/market.ts`, with its plausibility test `src/sim/models/market.test.ts` and its numbers in `src/data/market.ts` (grading and disposal are in `src/data/storage.ts`). It calls the household model's `demand` and `townDemand` for what households want (no second demand model), the storage model for ageing, the van's round and the waste's footprint (`docs/systems/storage.md`), and the energy model for diesel. Nothing calls it yet: the `boxScheme` and `farmShop` systems are not in `src/sim/systems.ts`, so it changes no pacing until part 14 wires it.

- **Role reversal** (`schemeDemand`, `boxPromise`): the household that buys from the shop at the garden is, at the smallholding, the customer. The scheme's subscribers are households by income decile (`Scheme.counts`, lowest first; box schemes skew to the upper middle), and their demand is `household.ts`'s: `schemeDemand(counts)` is `townDemand`, and N subscribers of a decile demand exactly what `demand(decile, N)` says. A box promises a set share (0.4) of one household's weekly veg by group, so a box is about 3 kg and a full box costs about £6.40 at the box price.
- **The box scheme** (`fillWeek`, `nextGoodwill`, `nextMonth`, `step`): each week, forty boxes ask for about 122 kg. The packer takes more raw kg than it delivers, for what the standard grades out and what the van's ride spoils, gives each group what the store holds, and finds the shortfall. Revenue is the box price times what's in the boxes (a short box is paid for by what it holds); a scheme that tops up (`topUp`) fills the gap from a wholesaler at 95 % of shop prices, which earns nothing and carries the import's carbon (Poore & Nemecek, air freight included). Goodwill moves toward the share of the promise delivered by 0.3 a week; every fourth week the subscribers change: a few leave (4 % a month, plus 0.25 for each point of goodwill short of 1, more if the box has crept up in price) and a few join (a base of one plus 3 % of the scheme by word of mouth, scaled by goodwill squared and how cheap the box looks). Forty boxes' first week is about £258, of which £2.75 is diesel.
- **What a short box costs** (a strategic trade-off, both ways): not topping up saves the import's small loss now and costs subscribers later. Over a month it is cheaper; over two years, at 85 % of the promise, topping up earns more, because goodwill holds and the scheme keeps its subscribers. At 70 % without top-ups the scheme loses a quarter of its subscribers in a year.
- **The farm shop** (`serveDay`, `footfallMean`, `customersToday`, `restock`): customers a day are a Poisson draw from the game's dice around a mean (30 in high summer, by season and weekday, more at weekends, fewer as the price lever rises), spread by the day's luck (weather and chance, a 35 % spread). Each customer is a household of a decile who buys a quarter of a week's veg at the shop's price, along `household.ts`'s elasticity (dearer, fewer kg), up to what the shelf has; the rest walk to a supermarket. Its takings a week are about £880 with unlimited stock (23 customers a day, 278 kg a week) but swing with season and luck. Unsold produce stays on the shelf and is aged by the storage system: salad is gone in about a week and a half, potatoes are not.
- **A price lever with no best setting** (test): with a short shelf a dearer shop takes more; with a plentiful one a cheaper shop does, because footfall and quantity both rise. The steady box against the shop's price and footfall is the same trade: the box's revenue varies far less week to week (a coefficient of variation a third or less of the shop's), but earns less a kg (0.9 of shop prices against 1.25, seasonal and by freshness).
- **Grading** (`grade`, `dispose`, `standardPremium`): a strict cosmetic standard (WRAP: farms reject a tenth to a quarter of some crops for looks) grades out about three times what a loose one does, roughly 15 to 22 % against 5 to 8 %, and earns 12 % more a kg. The graded-out share goes to waste, compost or feed (`dispose`: carbon, land and value; landfill methane against composting). With plenty picked, strict raises price a kg and waste together and earns more; with too little picked, it earns less. The waste is the loop: cosmetic standards and cheap food make waste and lose land.
- **Seasonal price** (`seasonal`, `pricePerKg`, `holdValue`): price is the national average times the lever, times a season (dearer when the produce is scarce: potatoes peak in late spring, salad, tomatoes and greens in winter) and a freshness factor (a lot keeps its price until it turns). `holdValue` compares selling today with holding: potatoes from autumn to spring gain because the price rises more than the loss; salad held ten days loses whether cold or not. Neither always wins: warm potatoes held a year lose.
- **The systems** (`boxScheme`, `farmShop`): `boxScheme` runs each `week` on the node with a `scheme` lever, taking from the `store` node: the delivered kg go to `sold`, the graded-out and road-spoiled kg to the store's waste, the subscribers' money to the purse, the imports' cost out to `bought` and their carbon to the air, the diesel through `burn` on the box node; then it steps the scheme and keeps a `ledger`. `farmShop` runs each `day` on the node with a `shop` lever and a `target` lever (kg by group on the shelf): the store restocks the shelf up to the target (the graded-out share to the store's waste), customers buy, and the takings go to the purse. Both refuse a plan on their `ledger`. Chance is only the shop's customers and their deciles, drawn from the tick's `rng`.
- **Fast effect** this week's box filled or short and a day's sales. **Slow effect** goodwill, and so the subscribers over months, and the price and waste a standard sets across a season.
- **Speed** (Node 22, the session's 4-core 2.1 GHz Xeon, from the test's timer): a shop day about 37 µs, a 40-box week's fill a few tens of µs, the storage model's day about 23 µs: a smallholding's day (a store, a van, a shop and a 40-box scheme) about 60 µs, far inside the parts-5-to-14 reserve.

## Wiring

**Part 14 (the first market and the road):**
- Nodes: `store` and `shop` (see `storage.md`), and `box` (`BOX_NODE`, `kind: 'box'`) with levers `scheme` (`Scheme`, from `startScheme(n)`) and `ledger` (`Ledger`); the shop node has `shop` (`Shop`, from `startShop()`), `target` (`Kg` by group) and `ledger` (`ShopLedger`). The purse is `PURSE` in `energy.ts` (the kitchen's `money`). Stocks: in store (`store`'s `food.<product>`), on the shelf (`shop`'s), and the van's is inside `fillWeek`. Flows: `box scheme` and `farm shop` (kgFood to `sold`, £ from `sold` to the purse), `restocking the shop` (store to shop), `graded out and lost on the road` and `going off` (kgFood to `decay`, kgWaste to the store's `waste`), `top-up imports` (£ to `bought`, kg CO₂e from `bought` to the air), `delivery round` (energy.ts's diesel). Boundaries: `sold` (customers), `bought` (imports, diesel), `grid` (the cold store), `decay`: no new ones. Levers the player sets: the box price (`scheme.priceIndex`), whether to top up (`scheme.topUp`), the box's grading standard (`scheme.standard`), the shop's price (`shop.priceIndex`) and standard (`shop.standard`), the shop's target stock, and how many subscribers to seek (the counts move by themselves). `Boundary` in `graph.ts` needs no addition.
- Systems: add `boxScheme` and `farmShop` to `src/sim/systems.ts` after `storage` (`storage.md`), each with one line. The round's day is the `week` tick's day; if the bot needs a different day, give `Scheme` a `day` field and check `c.date.weekday` in `boxScheme`. The two systems find their nodes by id and do nothing without them.
- Commands: the levers above are `plan` commands on the `box` and `shop` nodes (`Scheme` and `Shop` are replaced whole; the setter's pure half is `{...scheme, priceIndex}`); the ledgers are kept, not set.
- The map: the van on the road for the round's hours (drawn from the `delivery round` flow), boxes on doorsteps as subscribers' homes get theirs, the shop's queue (its customer count, one figure a stopped-at-the-door line), spoiled produce dulled on the shelf and carried to the heap, graded-out produce to the compost or the pigs. With reduced motion, the boxes appear at the doors and the van's round is a line.
- Explain cards: why a box scheme is steadier (a contract and a steady price against footfall and weather), why a farm shop earns more a kg, Q10 spoilage, where food is wasted (`storage.md`), what cosmetic grading wastes (WRAP) and what imports cost in carbon.
- Bot milestones it shifts: the first sale beyond the honesty box (`shop.ledger.firstSale`), the first box (`ledger.firstBox`) and the first short box (`ledger.firstShort`) are new; the honesty box's sales drop as the shop takes the stock; money grows faster once boxes pay (about £250 a week for forty boxes against the purse's £80 a week, so #21 and #22 need re-baselining).
- Saved state adds the `box` node's `scheme` and `ledger` levers, the `shop` node's `shop`, `target` and `ledger` levers, and (from `storage.md`) the `keeping` levers; nothing else. The version rises and an older save starts a new game. Levers are JSON: `Scheme` and `Shop` hold plain numbers and strings.
- The kitchen's honesty box and the shop share the store's stock: part 14 decides whether the gate's stock is a slice of the store or its own node.
- The labour model: `boxHours` gives hours for packing and driving; part 14 turns them into two `labour.ts` jobs (`WorkJob`) that `fit` assigns to workers, and refusing hours is a short box.

**Level 5 (the market town):** `fillWeek` and `serveDay` take `Stock` and counts, not a farm, so a town has many stores, routes and shops: one `Scheme` per farm with its own goodwill, the shops' customers drawn from `townDemand` over a town's income deciles, and grading standards set at the retailer (the third reversal: the player sets the rules). The seasonal price and the import fill are the market's; a farm's share of the town's demand is what the sealed smallholding carries up.

## How it fits and grows

1. **Born where:** hands-on at the smallholding: the box scheme's customers, the van, the shop and the store, moving kg, £, kWh and kg CO₂e, conserved (every kg picked is sold, eaten, spoiled or composted).
2. **Across the ladder:** a sealed smallholding carries up its market share (its subscribers and takings), its Freshness at the door and its losses; at the market town it is one of many suppliers; at the region its share is an average. The role reversal: buyer at the garden, seller here, the market at level 5, where the player later buys, then sets the rules.
3. **Loops:** the waste loop (cosmetic standards and cheap food → waste → methane and lost land) and the energy loop (the cold store's power price, the van's diesel). Fast: this week's box and a day's sales. Slow: goodwill and subscribers over months, and a season's waste.
4. **People:** a person packs the boxes (5 minutes a box, 3.3 hours for forty), a person drives the round (3 hours), a person serves in the shop (a 7-hour day, 4 minutes a customer); the player does them or hires or asks the partner (`labour.ts`); the shop's price and the box's promise are the player's.
5. **The lever:** the box price and its promise (the share), the shop price, the grading standards, topping up, the shop's target stock and the cold store; "let them decide" is a manager's, later, and the recommendations show the cost each way.
6. **The map:** the van, the boxes on doorsteps, the shop's queue, produce dulling and going to the heap; reduced motion shows the boxes at the doors and a line for the round.
7. **Explain:** the mechanism and source for each effect: Q10 spoilage (`storage.md`), where food is wasted, the cold chain's energy, why a box scheme is steadier, price elasticities (Green et al.), seasonal prices (DEFRA), grading (WRAP).
8. **Economy and balance:** about £258 a week from forty boxes (122 kg, £6.40 a box), the shop £880 a week at full stock, £2.75 of diesel a round, 3.3 packing hours and 3.1 driving hours a week; wiring shifts the purse (money milestones) and adds the first sale beyond the gate.
9. **Carbon and land:** the round's 8.7 kg CO₂e, the imports' carbon by group, the methane and land of graded-out food (`dispose`) and the cold store's electricity.
10. **Polish:** the box panel is a list of subscribers by decile, a goodwill bar and the week's fill as a strip that wraps at 320 px; the shop's takings as a small chart beside the map on large screens; concise UK English ("box full", "3 short", "goodwill 0.8").
11. **The lesson:** a steady buyer is worth a lower price; most food waste is at home and on the farm, not in shops; a strict cosmetic standard trades price against waste.
12. **Unfolding:** the box scheme's goodwill shows with the first short box (`market.goodwill`), the price lever and footfall with the first farm-shop sale (`market.shop`), grading with the first graded-out kg (`market.grading`), and the box scheme itself with the first box: proposed `unfold.ts` keys, not added.

## Numbers and what they simplify

Prices, baskets and elasticities come from the household model (DEFRA Family Food, ONS, Green et al.); churn, joining, the box's share, footfall and its seasons, the import price and the premium are rounded orders of magnitude from box-scheme and farm-retail surveys, not precise figures. Every household of a decile is the same; goodwill is one number; joiners and leavers are expected values, not people; the shop's customers are Poisson draws around a mean spread by a day's luck, and each buys a fixed share of a week's veg; unmet demand is lost; the wholesaler's price is one share of shop prices; a strict standard's premium applies to what's sold, not to the demand.
