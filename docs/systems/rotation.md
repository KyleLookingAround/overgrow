# Rotation

A year plan over fields, what came before on each, and soil at field scale, per hectare: `src/sim/models/rotation.ts` (its plausibility test `src/sim/models/rotation.test.ts`) from the data in `src/data/rotation.ts`, built on `soil.ts`'s functions (`hydraulics`, `humified`, `tempFactor`, `moistFactor`), `machinery.ts`'s compaction (`wheelings`, `recovered`, `yieldFactor`, `compactedStructure`), `pests.ts`'s soil-borne numbers (`SOILBORNE` in `src/data/pests.ts`) and the flowers' bees (`src/data/flowers.ts`). Written ahead of part 11 (fields and the year plan), which wires it; nothing in the game calls it yet, and nothing here is listed in `src/sim/systems.ts`. Issue #43; brief `docs/briefs/rotation-and-fields.md`.

## How it works

`farmYear(field, plan, weather)` runs one field through one agricultural year (September to August) and returns a new field and a `Result`; the input is never changed. A `Field` is plain per-hectare numbers: carbon in humus and fresh matter, nitrogen as organic, residue (last year's leftovers and manure, the next crop's credit) and mineral nitrate, phosphorus, potassium, compaction, soil-borne disease by family, the last five years grown, whether a catch crop is in and the share of the field in margins. A `Plan` is an act (a crop: wheat, barley, potatoes, beans, rape; or `cover`, `ley`, `fallow`), plus optional manure (t/ha), a catch crop after harvest, a `feed` share of the advised nitrogen and whether to `maintain` phosphorus and potassium.

- **Organic matter** is soil.ts's RothC in spirit, annualised: humus decays at 0.02 a year and fresh matter at 1, scaled by the temperature and moisture modifiers and slowed by living cover (0.4 of the share of the year the ground is green); of what decays, `humified(clay)` (about a fifth) becomes humus and the rest goes to the air. New residues, roots, manure and catch crop go in as fresh matter and start decaying next year, so a cover crop's carbon pays back over years. The nitrogen decay frees (C:N of 10) becomes nitrate.
- **Nitrogen** (RB209's supply and demand): the season's supply is the winter's leftover nitrate, what mineralisation and last year's residues free, and manure's first-year share; the advised fertiliser fills what a crop's need (recovered at 70 %) leaves short, up to RB209's maximum; the crop takes what it can, and its yield is `1 − 0.8 × (1 − share of need met)`, as `crops.ts` does. A legume fixes most of its own and leaves 60 % of its residue nitrogen as next year's credit (the rest joins the organic matter); a cover crop and a ley leave more. Each year conserves nitrogen: fertiliser, manure and fixation in; the harvest, leaching and nitrous oxide out; the stocks gain the difference (a test checks it for every act).
- **Leaching and nitrous oxide.** The winter's drainage flushes the rooting zone (0.9 m): the share of mineral nitrate lost is `1 − exp(−drainage / water held)`, except what a crop in the ground over winter (autumn-sown wheat and rape, a cover crop, a ley, or a catch crop) holds. Nitrous oxide is the IPCC's: 1 % of applied and residue nitrogen, and 1.1 % of what leaches, as N₂O-N, times 44/28 and 273 for kg CO₂e.
- **What came before.** A soil-borne disease per family (`FIELD_SOILBORNE`: the garden's clubroot and potato cyst nematode, a field's build-up faster, and a cereal's take-all added) builds when a crop of the family finishes and halves over its half-life without a host; yield loses `harm × inoculum`. So wheat on wheat loses about 4 % in year two and 15 % in year three, and potatoes on potatoes far more; a break sets it back. Wheels: each crop's jobs compact the field by `machinery.wheelings()` on the ground's wetness that day (potato lifting on wet autumn ground the worst), and a year takes it down by `recovered()`. Compaction cuts yield by up to 30 % and structure with it.
- **Soil health, water and yield.** `healthOf()` is soil.ts's 4:3:3 of organic matter, structure (less compaction) and moisture stress; yield takes up to 10 % off for poor health, and a dry summer takes up to 35 % off less for a soil that holds more water (Saxton & Rawls through `hydraulics()`, so organic matter pays back in a drought). Phosphorus and potassium are one pool each in kg/ha that the harvest draws down and the plan puts back (by default); below index 1 yield falls (up to 50 %).
- **A margin** (a hedge or flower strip) takes its share of the field, lifts bees to the flower model's numbers (pollination for beans and rape) and holds back generic pests by up to half: it pays a bean field and costs a cereal one.
- **The score** (`scorePlans()`): a plan (a list, or a chooser given the field as it stands, like `followed`, "follow the rotation": beans, wheat, potatoes, barley) over some years gives the margin by year and in all, nitrate leached, nitrous oxide, soil carbon change, and the end organic matter, disease, compaction and health. The tests read each trade-off both ways: a four-course rotation loses year one to continuous wheat or potatoes (a legume's cash is lowest) and beats both over three years (about £2,560 a hectare against £2,310 for continuous wheat and £2,520 for continuous potatoes, with a fraction of the disease and less compaction), and by year five continuous potatoes are losing money on a quarter of a crop; a cover crop costs about £90 and a season and puts 2.6 t C in (about 0.02 points of organic matter more after three years than a wheat), and never earns its cash back in three; fallow earns nothing and eases disease and wheels but loses the most carbon and leaches; a ley builds the most carbon and earns a little.
- **Fast effect:** this year's yield and cash and the nitrogen a legume before it left. **Slow effect:** disease building over repeats, organic matter and structure changing over years, compaction and leached nitrate carrying on.

## Scale-free

Every function works per hectare and never reads a field's area, so a garden bed (0.0003 ha), a field and a farm give the same answer per hectare; `totalsOf(field, hectares)` turns it into a node's stocks in kg, and `summed(fields, results)` weights fields by area for a farm's or region's organic matter, health, yield, margin, leaching and nitrous oxide. A region uses the summed figures as its tint and one line. The garden's beds can run `farmYear` too (a bed a field of 3 m²), but part 6c keeps them on the day-by-day model.

## On the graph (for wiring)

`fieldOf(node)` reads a field from a node (`land.crops` and `land.grass` its area; soil.ts's stocks per hectare; a `nitrogen.residue` stock; a `texture` lever; a `past` lever for history, disease, catch crop, margin and compaction), `planOf(node, field)` its plan (the `plan` lever: an act, a `Plan`, or `'rotation'`), and `growYear(ctx, node)` runs the year as flows (decay and humification to the air, mineralisation, fertiliser and manure bought, leaching to drainage, nitrous oxide to the air as kg CO₂e, uptake and residues via growth, money to and from a `payer` lever) and replaces the levers; a test checks the stocks end where `farmYear` says and the flows balance. `rotation` is the `System`: on the `year` tick every `field` node grows a year.

## Speed

`farmYear` takes about 2.6 µs a field in Node 22 (a 2.1 GHz Xeon): ten fields a year in about 0.026 ms, over the whole game year, not a day.

## Wiring

For part 11:

- **Nodes, stocks and levers.** Add a `field` node kind with soil.ts's stocks (`startingSoil()` per field, `carbon` humus, `carbon.fresh`, `nitrogen.organic`, `nitrate`, `phosphorus`, `potassium`), and a `nitrogen.residue` stock (kg N, define the key in `graph`-side data; the unit is `kgN`); the levers `plan`, `past`, `texture`, `payer`. Edges must carry `kgCO2e` to the atmosphere. No new unit; **no new boundary**: leaching goes to `drainage`, uptake and residues through `growth`, nitrous oxide's nitrogen to `decay` and its CO₂e into the air, inputs to `bought`, produce money from `sold`.
- **System.** List `rotation` in `src/sim/systems.ts`. It runs on the `year` tick; the crop's produce is not stocked: `growYear` pays `revenue − cost` from the payer's money, so part 11 decides whether harvests go to `kgFood` stocks (and through the market, part 14) or stay money, and takes the seasons' hours from `labour.ts`.
- **Weather.** `farmYear` takes `{drainage, dry, season}`: build it from the weather model's year (winter rain less evaporation, a dry summer's stress score); the default is a mean year.
- **Machinery.** Fuel per operation and breakdowns stay with `machinery.operate()`; `farmYear` uses only its compaction functions and the used tractor's weight. If a field is worked by hand or by a hired contractor, pass the wheelings in the act's data.
- **Commands.** `plan` is a lever set through `plan`, as every change to the game is: an act, an act with manure, a catch crop or `'rotation'`. "Let them decide" is the adviser (the agency model) choosing.
- **Margins** are `Field.margin`; wiring it to `biodiversity.ts` means feeding the same visits (`BEES` numbers are used here directly).
- **Proposed `unfold.ts` keys** (not added): `rotation.plan` (the year plan's line and "follow the rotation") when the smallholding's first field is bought (a first field record), `soil.nitrogen` (the nitrogen and organic matter numbers) on the first feeding choice (the first advised fertiliser or manure), `rotation.cover` (cover crops, ley and fallow) when a soil-borne disease is first noted (a cause named like clubroot or take-all), and `soil.leaching` (nitrate leached and nitrous oxide) once the first winter's leaching is recorded. GATES: `plan` acts `cover`, `ley` and `fallow` wait on `rotation.cover`.
- **Map.** Fields coloured by crop and season, a cover crop greening a winter field, ruts where compaction is high, bare fields tinted for leaching; reduced motion shows the seasons as steps.
- **Balance.** Wiring it moves the smallholding's income: a rotation earns about £850 a hectare a year in this model against a garden bed's cash; set the bot's smallholding milestones (part 11's balance run) with the year plan's first-year loss in mind, since the legume year earns least.

**Fixed** (Bug #47, #48, `docs/briefs/garden-nutrients.md`): crop residues now carry the P and K the harvest didn't take to the heap, compost carries them back to the beds, and the garden's soils start at RB209's index 4; year three now picks 67–99 % of year one's kg on seeds 1–3, and no bed runs out in four years (`src/sim/garden-years.test.ts`; `docs/systems/soil.md`, `carbon.md` and `crops.md`). The diagnosis as it was:

For part 6c, **the garden's beds' output falls towards nothing by year three** because of the nutrients, not organic matter, not the nitrogen and (mostly) not the pests. Run headless on `main` (seed 1; seeds 2 and 3 alike), with the garden's own gardener and plan:

- The kitchen's picked kg a year runs 31.4, 16.4, 5.8 and 8.1 in years 1 to 4 (seed 2: 31.0, 18.2, 8.1, 8.1; seed 3: 33.0, 12.7, 8.2, 6.3).
- **Phosphorus and potassium run out and nothing puts them back.** A dug bed of 3 m² holds soil.ts's index 2 pools: 0.018 kg P and 0.135 kg K (60 and 450 kg/ha in 30 cm). A salad crop takes 12 kg P and 100 kg K a hectare, beans 12 and 60, potatoes 25 and 220, tomatoes 25 and 250: three to five crops empty them. Bed-1 goes from P 0.018 to 0.010 kg and K 0.135 to 0.06 by the end of year one, to 0.002 and 0.00 in year two, and to zero after; bed-2 is at 0.003 and 0.01 after year one. Crop yield is then `1 − 0.8 × (1 − got/need)` with `got/need` near zero, so a fifth of a full crop. Nothing returns them: crop uptake goes to the `growth` boundary, the heap's stocks (`HEAPED` in `carbon.ts`) carry carbon and organic nitrogen only, and compost adds no P or K.
- **Proof.** Topping both up to their starting values at the start of year 3 lifts year 3 from 5.8 to 20.3 kg and year 4 from 8.1 to 14.9 (seed 2: 8.1 to 29.6 and 8.1 to 12.2; seed 3: 8.2 to 29.3). Also holding mineral nitrogen at 100 kg/ha lifts year 3 again to 28.4 (seed 2: 39.7).
- **Secondary causes.** Mineral nitrogen is thin (40 kg/ha at the start, 0.012 kg in a bed, against a salad's 90 kg/ha uptake): `nitrate` sits at about 0.01 kg and only mineralisation (about 190 kg N/ha a year from the humus, `orgN` 2.98 falling to 2.81 in three years) refills it, so the crops are nitrogen-limited too. Soil-borne disease is real in one bed: bed-2's clubroot inoculum sits at 0.9 from year one (salad, a brassica, every year), halving that bed's salad growth; bed-1's stays at 0.03–0.15. Organic matter is not the cause: bed health only falls from 87 to 82 over four years, and the beds' carbon from 109 to 102 kg CO₂e.
- **The fix belongs in the garden** (part 6c): bring P and K back as compost and manure flows (`HEAPED` needs `phosphorus` and `potassium`, and `carbon.ts`'s spreading needs to move them into the bed), return crop residue's nutrients to the heap instead of the `growth` boundary, and either raise the bed pools to what a few years' crops take or accept that buying a fertiliser is the first feeding choice. A garden's owner adds compost, which is the real mechanism. Nothing in this file edits `soil.ts`, `crops.ts` or `carbon.ts`.
- **This model doesn't share the fault:** its P and K are topped up by the plan (`maintain`, the default) and a test runs eight years of the rotation holding them within 10 % of the start; `maintain: false` mines them and yield falls to 85 % of the kept plan by year eight, the same fault shown on purpose as a trade-off.

## How it fits and grows

1. **Born where.** The garden's beds (a family not twice running, the dig-and-plan of part 3 with the garden's soil-borne pests), hands-on at the smallholding's fields (level 3): each field a node with soil stocks; flows are kg of N, P and K, kg of carbon in organic matter, kg N₂O and nitrate leached, conserved.
2. **Across the ladder.** A sealed smallholding carries its soil in Health and its rotation in Reliability (`summed()` gives the organic matter and health, and the yields' year-to-year spread); at the farm (4) many fields' plans; at the region, soil and nitrate as a tint and one line; it comes back as policy (nitrate zones, soil standards).
3. **Loops.** The intensification loop (inputs now, soil down over years: `feed` and `maintain`, and the compaction) and the carbon loop (organic matter as a sink, N₂O as an emission).
4. **People.** The smallholder and the hand do the work (hours through `labour.ts`); the player sets the year plan or lets the adviser.
5. **The lever.** The year plan: `plan` per field per year; "follow the rotation"; "let them decide".
6. **The map.** As under Wiring.
7. **Explain.** Nitrogen fixation (RB209), soil-borne disease (AHDB), organic matter (RothC), leaching and N₂O (IPCC), each with its source.
8. **Economy and balance.** `scorePlans()` gives the kg and £ each plan earns over three years.
9. **Carbon and land.** Organic matter as a sink, N₂O as an emission, margins taking land for wildlife.
10. **Polish.** The year plan's panel at 320 px: one row a field with its act, a follow-the-rotation switch; concise UK English.
11. **The lesson.** Rotation and cover pay back over years, not in the first one; the same crop every year wins once and loses after.
12. **Unfolding.** As proposed above.
