# Water

The FAO-56 soil water balance for each bed and the lawn, and the butt filling from the shed's roof (`src/sim/models/water.ts`, with its plausibility test in `src/sim/models/water.test.ts`), on the soils of `src/sim/models/soil.ts` and the roof in `src/data/garden.ts`; the panel shows a place's moisture (`src/ui/Panel.tsx`) and the map colours the dug beds by it (`src/ui/map/draw.ts`).

- **Each step** (the `hour` tick) the water model takes the step's rain (`rainBetween()`) and its share of the day's reference evapotranspiration (`et0()` times `lightBetween()`: none at night), or at a step of a day or more each day's whole rain and evapotranspiration in turn (from the weather's `step` at a week or a month), and for every place with soil moves, in order: rain in from the `rain` boundary; anything above saturation out to `runoff`; evapotranspiration out to `evapotranspiration`; and a share of what's above field capacity out to `drainage`, half of it in about eight hours and the last half millimetre at once, no faster than the saturated conductivity, with the nitrate it carries (the soil model's `leach()`). Every litre is a flow, so the `conservation` test covers it.
- **Reference evapotranspiration**: FAO Penman-Monteith on the day's maximum, minimum and sunshine, with FAO-56's missing-data rules (dew point at the minimum in a humid climate, wind 2 m/s, radiation from sunshine hours by Ångström); Hargreaves on temperature alone for a day with no sunshine figure. Both are in the file and the test holds them within 40 % of each other on a June day. A hot sunny July day gives 4–6 mm, a January day under 1 mm, and a year at the station about 600–700 mm.
- **The ground's coefficient** (`groundCoefficient()`): grass at FAO-56's cool-season turf (0.9) with its water stress coefficient once half its available water is gone, and bare soil evaporating at up to 1.05 until the surface's readily evaporable water is gone and then less, stopping once its total evaporable water is (FAO-56's REW and TEW, by texture in `src/data/soils.ts`), each in proportion to its share of the place's land. So a bare bed stays moist below a dry crust while grass dries deeper. Part 3 adds the crops' coefficients here, by crop and stage, for the ground they cover.
- **The butt.** The shed's roof (3.9 m², about 85 % of its rain reaching the gutter) drains into the butt; above its 200 L cap the rest overflows to `runoff`. Nothing takes water out of it yet: the gardener's can (part 3) will.
- **Its speed.** With the soil and the weather, about 0.17 ms a garden game day headless (185 flows a day, most of them the hourly evapotranspiration), inside the part's 0.25 ms.
