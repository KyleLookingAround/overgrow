# The clock

One clock for every level, in game hours since the start (`src/sim/clock.ts`), with the ladder's rates in `src/data/ladder.ts`, the systems that run on it listed in `src/sim/systems.ts`, and the real-time loop on the page in `src/app/clock-loop.ts`, which starts its view afresh when a snapshot's level changes (the step up), as it does at a load.

- **Steps.** The sim advances in fixed steps of the level's `stepHours`: an hour at levels 1 and 2, a day at 3 to 5, a week at 6 and 7, a month at 8. A `tick` command runs whole steps (any part of a step left over is dropped).
- **The calendar.** Day 1 is 06:00 on Monday 15 March, early spring, on a UTC calendar so every date repeats. `calendar(hours)` gives the date, weekday, time, day of the year, season (meteorological: spring from 1 March) and the game year, counted from 1 and turning on the anniversary of the start. The top bar shows it.
- **Ticks.** Each step fires `hour`, and `day`, `week`, `season` or `year` when it crosses midnight, Monday midnight, the first of March, June, September or December, or the anniversary (`ticksCrossed()`). A system handles the ticks it needs; an hourly system at level 3 is called once a day with `dt` 24.
- **Systems.** A system is its own file exporting a `System` (`name`, `on` by tick, and optionally `command` for the commands it owns), listed in `src/sim/systems.ts` in the order they run. Adding one adds a line there and edits no other system. A system may name the `levels` it runs at: the garden's run at level 1 only and the allotment's at level 2 (`docs/systems/allotment.md`), and `runStep()` and the commands skip the rest. Each gets its own dice every tick (`systemRng()`, from the seed, its name, the tick and the hour), so adding a system never changes another's draws. Today they are the weather, the water, the soil, the biodiversity, the pests, the crops, the gardener, the kitchen and the carbon of the heap, in that order (`docs/systems/weather.md`, `docs/systems/water.md`, `docs/systems/soil.md`, `docs/systems/biodiversity.md`, `docs/systems/pests.md`, `docs/systems/crops.md`, `docs/systems/gardener.md`, `docs/systems/kitchen.md`, `docs/systems/carbon.md`).
- **The pace: the zoom is the speed** (`docs/decisions/ADR-2026-09-30-zoom-is-the-speed.md`). There are no speed buttons: the `speed` command only pauses (0) and runs (1), and is saved (`SPEEDS`). How fast a running game goes comes from the camera (`src/ui/map/camera.ts`): each level's `secondsPerDay` is a day's length with the camera at its widest view (the garden 12 s, the allotment 6 s), and `secondsPerDayAt(level, t)` goes from it to the level below's as the camera goes in (`t` 0 to 1), in log space, so each pinch changes the pace by the same factor; the page tells the loop with `setZoom(t)`. `hoursPerSecond(level, run, secondsPerDay)` is 24 ÷ that × `run`. With its quiet nights the garden's year is at most about 53 minutes, less the skips taken. The checks can run the clock faster with `window.__sim.clock(k)`, never a player's control.
- **The real-time loop** (`src/app/clock-loop.ts`) turns real seconds into steps on the page. It keeps the sim up to two steps ahead of what's shown and moves a view time between the two snapshots either side of it, which the map interpolates at. Pausing freezes the view at once; under `prefers-reduced-motion` the view jumps from snapshot to snapshot. A gap between frames longer than 0.25 s counts as 0.25 s, and a hidden tab gets no frames, so the game doesn't run while the page is closed or hidden. At a fast pace on a slow page (a skip's time-lapse, the checks' fast clock) the sim is kept two frames' worth ahead instead of two steps, so it keeps up at a few frames a second. If a command moves the sim more than four steps ahead, past what the loop itself asked for (the checks do), the view jumps to it.
- **Quiet nights** (`src/ui/quiet-night.ts`, the loop's `setQuiet`). At the levels that step by the hour, once it's dark and the gardener has gone to bed until dawn (after any dusk patrol), with no slug out and no frost on the map and no card or notice waiting but its own, the page tells the loop the night is quiet up to 06:00, and the loop runs it at `QUIET_BOOST` (4) times the zoom's pace, at most `QUIET_MOST` (64) game hours a second: under the about 77 the worker gives on a phone at 4× CPU throttling (the `scene` check). It hands back at dawn, or the moment something happens (a card, a notice, a slug, a frost); pause still pauses. The view's `quiet` says so: a moon shows on Pause ("Quiet night: passing quickly"), still under reduced motion, and the map no longer dims further: a quiet night passes fast enough that the map holds the steady light (`docs/systems/map.md`), darkening to the night's own dim (`--map-night-quiet`) only once the quiet night has lasted a real second, which in practice only a long one in the garden does. The first quiet night on a device says "Quiet nights pass quickly" once. It's pacing on the page only: the sim runs the same steps, so play is unchanged. On seeds 1–3 with the sensible bot's choices, about 8½ quiet hours a night make the garden's year about 53 minutes at its pace, less the skips taken. From level 3 the step is a day, so a night passes inside one tick.
- **Skips** (`src/sim/skip.ts`; the one map's spec, item 7). At levels 1 to 3 (`SKIP.levels`), where the camera can't pull out far enough to hurry time, the sim's foresight (`dueOf`, the snapshot's `due`) names what's next that needs the player, at least a day off and at most a fortnight (`SKIP.mostDays`): a crop ready in a bed (its degree days still to go at the month's normal warmth) or a bed's next sowing, at 07:00 that morning, or "a fortnight on" if nothing comes sooner; at the allotment, Monday's new week. None is offered while a frost is forecast on tender crops, while down in a rescue, or where it's under a day off. The `skip` command sets `State.skip` to an hour no later than that; the tick ends it at its hour or at the first wake (`wakeOf`): a cause in `WAKES` (a frost on a crop, blight weather, the allotment's slug outbreak), a frost forecast on tender crops, or something newly unfolded (a pest's first sighting among them), saying why in `woke`. The loop runs it as a time-lapse of about `SKIP.seconds` (2.5) whatever its length, never faster than the worker's steps come back (under reduced motion as fast as they do), every hour simulated as normal, so a skipped stretch ends where a watched one does (`src/sim/skip.test.ts`). It isn't saved: a save taken during one keeps the hour reached. The page offers it as a chip (`docs/systems/map.md`); the bot, which plays without a clock, never skips.
