# The household economy

Who lives in the household, their jobs, hours and wages, the weekly shop, what shop food carries in carbon, land and water, the purse, and the same baskets summed over many households, written ahead of part 6b and wired into the garden by it (`src/sim/models/household.ts`, with its plausibility test in `src/sim/models/household.test.ts` and its numbers in `src/data/household.ts`; the `household` system runs after the kitchen in `src/sim/systems.ts`, and the gardener (`src/sim/gardener.ts`) and the kitchen (`src/sim/models/kitchen.ts`) take their hours and their ask from it). The labour model (`src/sim/models/labour.ts`, `docs/systems/labour.md`) has merged; I read it and reused nothing, because its `Role` (owner, hired hand, seasonal picker), its season-by-season hours and its wage by role describe employed farm work, where a household member's job is elsewhere and a weekday's hours are a flat eight. They should share what's the same: the `person` node with a `worker` lever and `hours` stock in `h` for a member who works on the farm (its `goals.hoursCap` is the hook for a partner's agency later), the `time` boundary that gives and takes hours, and `labour`'s owner hours, which at the smallholding replace `gardenHours` for the owner (ten hours in summer, six in winter). Part 13 folds the two: a household member who farms is a `Worker` whose hours come from `labour`, and whose wage is the household's `weekWage` when they work off the farm.

- **Members** (`Household`, `Member`): a role (gardener, partner, child), a job (none, part-time, full-time, or school for a child) and the work-or-help lever `workShare`, 0–1, the share of the job's hours worked. A new game's household is the gardener, full-time (`startHousehold()`).
- **Hours** (`workHours`, `commuteHours`, `freeHours`, `gardenHours`): a full-time job is eight hours at work on a weekday plus an hour's commute (ASHE, DfT); free hours are the 16-hour waking day less two for essentials, the job and the commute; a role gives the garden a share of them, the rest being the rest of life. The gardener's shares (0.8 weekdays, 3/7 weekends) give four garden hours on a weekday and six at a weekend, what `src/data/jobs.ts`'s `HOURS` held before part 6b, so wiring it changed no garden hours (the gardener takes them from here now). Part-time is three days.
- **Wages** (`dayWage`, `weekWage`): take-home pay by the hour at work, £590 for 40 hours (about ASHE's median full-time, after tax). Hours moved from the job to the garden cost wage and add garden hours: a partner on full-time work gives about 12 garden hours a week, working half they give about 17, helping full-time about 23; the gardener going part-time gives up two fifths of the wage for about 14 more.
- **The commute** (`commute`): the hour a member leaves through the gate and the hour they return on a day worked (8:30, back at 17:30 when full-time), or null. The map draws it.
- **The basket** (`basket`, `shop`): kg a person a week by food group, veg by the kitchen's four groups and the rest of the diet in five (fruit, bread and cereals, dairy and eggs, meat and fish, everything else), at Family Food prices; about £30 a person a week. `shop(need, supplied)` returns what the shop buys, its cost, the **groceries saved** (the garden's kg at shop prices, up to what the household needed) and the spare (over the need, for the honesty box). A garden meeting the whole veg need saves about £6.50 a person a week.
- **The kitchen's ask** (`kitchenAsk(people)`, kept in `src/sim/models/kitchen.ts` so the kitchen needn't import this file, and re-exported here): the basket's veg over seven days. For 2.4 people it's 0.98 kg a day, the old fixed ask's 1.0 with every group within 10 %. The garden's is the gardener's alone, 0.42 kg a day; a partner moving in doubles it to 0.84.
- **The footprint** (`footprint`, `footprintOf`): per kg, farm-to-shop carbon, land (m² a year) and fresh water (Poore & Nemecek), with the transport share of the carbon split out and an air-freighted share of some groups adding long-haul carbon (9 kg CO₂e a kg). A week's typical shop carries about 42 kg CO₂e a person (about 2.2 t a year), three quarters of it meat and dairy; transport is under 5 % of it. This is the myth the Explain card corrects: what you eat matters far more than how far it came.
- **The purse** (`purse`, `restSpend`): wages and sales in, the shop and one flat outgoing for the rest of life out (`REST_SPEND` by role: housing, bills, travel and the savings that aren't the garden's). The player's money is the gardener's budget for the garden, not the household's savings: a lone gardener nets about £80 a week, a household whose partner has stopped work goes down.
- **Many households** (`demand`, `townDemand`, `keptness`, `hoursPerPlot`): `demand(decile, n, price)` is the weekly kg, spend and income of `n` households of an ONS income decile (0 the lowest): the basket scaled by the decile's people and a mix that runs from the lowest decile's to the highest's (richer buys more fruit and meat), the price a flat multiple lower or higher, and quantities moved by price along a constant own-price elasticity by group (Green et al.: meat and fruit about −0.7, staples about −0.3) that's steeper for poorer deciles. It's linear in `n`, and `townDemand` sums deciles. The lowest decile spends about 14 % of its income on food, the highest about 5 % (Family Food's direction). An allotment neighbour is a household whose `hoursPerPlot` gives `keptness`, the share of a plot's six weekly hours it gets.
- **The system** (`household`): a `day` tick counts what the kitchen's last meal ate of the garden's produce by food group. The week's shop (`weeklyShop`) buys the basket less what the garden gave since the last shop and less what's still in the cupboard (`restock`), pays the week's wages and takes out the rest of life's spending, puts the food into the kitchen's cupboard (`shopFood`, from the `shop` boundary: two stocks, `food.shop-veg` and `food.shop-food` for the rest of the diet, shared out by group in the basket's proportions by `cupboardIn`, since the snapshot copies every stock each hour), notes the `groceries saved` (the garden's kg at the shop's prices), and carries the shop's carbon, land and water into the household node's `carbon`, `footprint.land` and `footprint.water` stocks, never into the air. The gardener does it on the way home from work on their last working day, Friday (`shopDay`, the `home` step's effect in `src/sim/gardener.ts`), so the bags are drawn coming in at the kitchen door; a `week` tick does it at Monday midnight for a household that went a week without (no one working its last day). The ledger keeps the running totals, the last shop's hour and the last week's shop for the Kitchen tab (`week`). It does nothing without a `household` node with a `ledger` lever, and refuses a plan on the ledger or on `members` (the job's hours are fixed until part-time at the smallholding, and the partner comes at part 10). It draws no dice: nothing here is a chance.
- **The cupboard** (`cupboardIn`, `cupboardDays`): the shop's food waits in the kitchen and is eaten a day at a time (the kitchen's meal, `docs/systems/kitchen.md`), so the Kitchen tab and the kitchen's place show the days of food in it; a new game starts with last week's shop in (`startCupboard`). This is #29's Q8 (d); Q8 (c), packaging that can't go on the heap, and (e), shop prices moving with the world's, are left for later: there's no bin flow or world price yet to hang them on (`shop()`'s `priceIndex` is the hook for (e)).
- **Speed** (Node 22, the session's 4-core 2.1 GHz Xeon, after a warm-up, from the test's timer): a day's garden hours for one household about 1 µs, and a town's demand over ten deciles about 30–70 µs; the weekly system tick a few microseconds. All are far inside the parts-5-to-14 reserve.

## Wiring

**Part 6b (the household), as built:** the node `household` (kind `household`, no box, `land.built` 0 because the house is the kitchen's strip on the map) with `footprint.land` and `footprint.water` beside its `carbon`, and the levers `members` and `ledger` (`householdNode()`); the boundaries `wages`, `shop` and `rest of life` in `Boundary`; the purse stays the kitchen's `money` (`PURSE`), which the top bar shows; the gardener's day's hours are `householdGardenHours` to the minute (`hoursOn` in `src/sim/gardener.ts`), so four on a weekday and six at the weekend as before; the commute is drawn from the gardener's steps (`leave`, `away` and `home`, which take none of the garden's hours, the map hiding them while `away`); the kitchen's ask is `kitchenAsk(people)`; the unfold keys are `household.commute`, `household.groceries` and `household.footprint`, with `garden.money` gaining `wages` as a cause (`docs/systems/unfolding.md`); and saved state is version 6. What it moved is in "Economy and balance" below.

**Part 6b, as proposed before it was built:**
- Add the node `household` beside the garden (kind `household`, its `land.built` the house's plot) with stocks `footprint.land` (m² a year) and `footprint.water` (L) beside the `carbon` every node has, and levers `members` (a `Member[]`, the plan lever; `withWorkShare`, `withJob` and `withMember` are its command's pure half) and `ledger` (`HouseholdLedger`). Add the boundaries `wages`, `shop` and `rest of life` to `Boundary` in `src/sim/graph.ts` (`HBoundary` names them), and an edge if the purse moves to the household node (`PURSE` names the kitchen's `money` today, where the honesty box pays; moving both is a one-line change). Add one line to `src/sim/systems.ts`, after `kitchen`: `household`.
- Replace `HOURS` in `src/data/jobs.ts` with `householdGardenHours(members, weekday)` in the gardener's day. Replace the kitchen's fixed `ASK` with `kitchenAsk(people(household))`. Have the shop's food land in the kitchen and be eaten (so the meal meets its ask from the shop's, not only the garden's): `weekFlows` deliberately moves only money and footprint. - Draw the commute (`commute`: the gardener through the gate at the hour, and back) and the shop's bags arriving on the weekly shop, each drawn from the flow. Propose `unfold.ts` keys: `household.purse` (the first wage or purchase), `household.groceries` (the first week the garden meets some of the shop), `household.footprint` (the first carbon choice, and the transport Explain card) and `household.commute` (the first weekday job).
- Saved state adds the `household` node's `members` and `ledger` levers and its three footprint stocks (nothing else); the version rises and an older save starts a new game (no compatibility before the first release).

**Part 8 (the allotment):** the eleven neighbours are households (`Household`s, a few with a partner, some with long hours); each plot's `keptness(hoursPerPlot(h))` sets how kept it is and so how it looks and what it gives. Their spend is not the player's.

**Part 10 (the partner):** `withMember(h, newMember('partner', …, 'partner', 'full'))` when the partner moves in; the lever is `withWorkShare(h, 'partner', 0…1)` (work, help or a mix), and "let them decide" is the agency system's, later. The kitchen's ask grows with `people(h)`, wages and the rest of life's outgoing with the member.

**Part 13 (the smallholding):** `withJob(h, 'gardener', 'part')` (or `workShare` below 1) is part-time; garden hours at scale come from `householdGardenHours`. A box scheme's customers (with part 14) are `demand(decile, n, price)` for the deciles of the town it serves, and the town's `townDemand` is the market the player later runs.

## How it fits and grows

1. **Born where:** hands-on at the garden, on the household node beside it, moving £ (the purse), hours (the members' garden hours) and kg of food bought, with carbon (kg CO₂e), land (m² a year) and water (L) carried in, conserved as flows from the `shop` boundary.
2. **Across the ladder:** a sealed garden carries up its household's garden hours a week, weekly spend and groceries saved; one level up the allotment's neighbours are households (part 8), two up the box scheme's customers are baskets (part 13); back as an aggregate, a town's demand by decile, and the role reversal: the player first sells to households, then runs the market they shop in.
3. **Loops:** it feeds the money loop (wage, shop and sales in the purse), the time loop (hours traded between job and garden) and the carbon loop. Fast: the week's shop and purse. Slow: the household's diet footprint, and time traded between job and garden.
4. **People:** the members, their hours and (later) goals; the player delegates by setting the lever or letting the partner decide, and the shop's mix is later a plan.
5. **The lever:** work, help or a mix (`workShare`); part-time; the shop's mix later; "let them decide" when agency lands.
6. **The map:** the gardener leaves through the gate at the hour and comes home, and shop bags arrive at the kitchen door on shop day, impact first; with reduced motion they appear at the door and the gate without the walk, and the purse shows only in its panel. As built: the gardener walks to the gate at 08:30, isn't drawn while away, and walks in from the gate to the kitchen at 17:30, carrying two bags on Friday.
7. **Explain:** hours (ASHE, DfT), wages (ASHE), the basket and prices (DEFRA Family Food), the footprint and the transport share (Poore & Nemecek), demand by decile (ONS, Family Food, Green et al.); the shop's footprint and the transport myth come first.
8. **Economy and balance:** about £590 a week in, £30 a person out on the shop and £480 on the rest of life, so the purse gains about £80 a week for a lone gardener; garden hours unchanged (four and six). Wiring it shifts the bot's purse and any milestone that reads money (#21 and #22 need re-baselining, and the money the gardener buys tools with is now the surplus, not a £20 start alone). As built (part 6b, the bot on seeds 1 to 3 through day 120): the first harvest doesn't move, and the kitchen's week half met holds (days 114 and 113, none on seed 1); the first sale comes on days 4 to 7, where before it never came in 120 days, because the kitchen's ask is now one person's 0.42 kg, not an average household's 1 kg, so a glut reaches the box (the founding spec's first minute wants it a minute after the first harvest); money is about £364 on day 30 and £1,430 on day 120; Output over the last 28 days falls (0.24 to 0.15 kg a day on the mean) because the kitchen keeps less and sells its glut sooner; wasted food rises a little. The weekly shop for one costs a little under the basket's £30, and groceries saved came to £14 to £16 over the 120 days against £2 to £9 taken at the box: veg is a small part of the bill, but what the garden feeds the household is worth more to it than what the box sells.
9. **Carbon and land:** the shop adds about 42 kg CO₂e a person a week beside the garden's own dial, never to it (a garden meeting its veg ask removes about 2 kg of that, since veg is light); the land is the shop's m² a year, an embedded account the land account counts in hectare-years and not as area on the map. As built: a bag and the footprint's kg sit under the dial's figure in the top bar (one control, so the bar keeps its row at 768 px), and the Kitchen tab has the last week's footprint and its transport share.
10. **Polish:** the panels list members and jobs in a list that wraps at 320 px; the purse and the week's shop sit as a simple table that becomes a chart beside the map on large screens; concise UK English ("groceries saved", "the shop", "work, help or a mix"). As built: the gardener's card has a line for their job once it unfolds, and the Kitchen tab a "Last week's shop" table (pay, the shop, the rest of life, groceries saved, food in the cupboard, its footprint); the chart and the members' list wait for the partner (part 10).
11. **The lesson:** growing your own saves money and carbon mostly through what it replaces (the shop's veg is light and cheap; meat and dairy are the bill and the carbon), and food miles are a small share of food's footprint.
12. **Unfolding:** the purse shows with the first wage or purchase, groceries saved with the first week the garden meets some of the shop, the shop's footprint with the first carbon choice, the commute with the first weekday job. As built: the purse is 6a's `garden.money`, now at the first payday (day 5); the commute (`household.commute`) is noted from the second day's homecoming, which lands in the same tick as the kitchen's first ask, so the first minute gets no sign of its own; `household.groceries` with the first shop the garden fed the household some of; `household.footprint` with the dial.

## Numbers and what they simplify

The footprint figures are Poore & Nemecek's global means read to about two figures and rounded, and the weighting of dairy and eggs and of meat and fish to a UK basket is this file's own; when 6b wires the model, check them against the supplementary tables. The basket's veg is the kitchen's NHS-portions ask, higher than Family Food's household purchases alone; the rest of life's outgoing is one flat number by role; a job is three days or five, with no overtime, sick days or holidays; prices are one national average with a flat multiple by decile. Partners and children have no goals or trust yet: the agency and neighbours models, later, add them.
