# Livestock

A herd or flock kept on a node: what it eats, drinks and makes, the manure and methane it gives off, its welfare and the chance it falls ill (`src/sim/models/livestock.ts`, with its plausibility test in `src/sim/models/livestock.test.ts`), from the species' figures in `src/data/livestock.ts`. Written ahead of the parts that wire it: part 6 (the garden's three hens) and part 12 (the smallholding's flock and pigs or sheep). Nothing is registered in `systems.ts` yet, so nothing ships.

- **Species** (`SPECIES`): laying hens, ewes, lambs, pigs (growers) and cattle, each with intake (kg dry matter a head a day), gain or lay rate, water, the temperatures it's comfortable between, space per head, how crowding hurts it, its enteric methane (IPCC Tier 1), nitrogen excreted (IPCC table 10.19) and manure. Poore & Nemecek's land, water and emissions per kg of product (`PER_KG`) are the diet question's numbers, for the Explain card. A ewe's lambs and a cow's milk are data only until part 12.
- **A day** (`step()`, pure, no graph): a herd (`Herd`, kept on its node as the `herd` lever, replaced each day) takes what grass its sward gives, then the node's `feed`, and drinks from its `water`. Hens lay `layRate` × the light (a floor of 0.4 at 8 hours, full from 13) × welfare's share × the cold's share × the feed and water met (a missed feed or an empty trough cuts today's eggs); growers gain up to finishing weight. Three hens on natural light lay about 265 eggs a year each, about a third as many a day in December as in June.
- **Manure and gases:** manure follows what was eaten: fresh mass, carbon (kg CO₂e of carbon) and nitrogen (IPCC's kg N per 1,000 kg a day). Enteric methane is IPCC's Tier 1 kg a head a year (sheep 8, cattle 57, pigs 1.5, poultry 0) at 27 times CO₂ (AR6). Manure is managed three ways: `heap` (droppings wait on the node until `clearOut()` moves them, with their carbon and nitrogen, to the heap; the heap's own gases are the carbon model's, on the manure's mass, so none are counted here), `spread` (methane conversion 0.1 %, nitrous oxide 1 % of the nitrogen, EF1) and `pasture` (1 %, and the species' EF3: 2 % for cattle, pigs and poultry, 1 % for sheep), the last two straight into the field's fresh carbon and organic nitrogen, with a tenth (spread) to 15 % (pasture) of the nitrogen lost to the air. `availableN()` is RB209's share a crop can use in the year (poultry 30 %, farmyard manure 10 %).
- **Welfare** (0–1, slow): heads toward space (crowding above the good density, and the ground's condition) × feed met × water met × warmth, falling over about 12 days and mending over 30. Below 0.8 it cuts eggs and gain, to nothing at 0.2. Overstocking also wears the ground bare (pigs fastest) and a sward grazed to the roots wears it further, over a season; grass grows to 0.012 kg/m² a day at 15 °C and above, scaled by the ground's condition.
- **Illness:** `diseaseRisk()` is the chance in a step (a species' yearly chance, up with density and down welfare), kept on the herd as `risk`; the system rolls it once a day from its own `Rng` (`fallsIll()`), and a sick herd makes half as much for 21 days, or 4 once `treat()`ed (part 12's vet). A herd with `disease: false` never falls ill.
- **Per kg** (`footprint()`): a lamb's methane and manure gases come to about 15 kg CO₂e a kg of meat (with its ewe's share), a third of Poore & Nemecek's 40 for the whole chain; a pig's about 0.6, an egg's about 0.16.
- **Speed:** the `day` tick only. Measured headless in Node 22 on the session's machine: about 3 µs a herd with its flows applied (a bare `step()` is under 1 µs), and 0.03 ms a game day for twelve herds (hens and ewes), so part 6 and part 12's few herds take a few per cent of the parts-5-to-14 reserve's 1.0 ms.

## Hooks for the levels above

Added for the owner's answers on #29 (all pure, none wired; the sealing maths' `GameEvent` is the only import from above):

- **Hours a head** (`hoursNeeded(herd, days)`, `SPECIES[…].hours`): the keeper's time, hours a head a day, up half while the herd is ill (`ILL_HOURS`); a hen about 1.8 hours a year, a ewe 7, a finishing pig 11 (about 3 over its four months), a cow 18 (Nix and the Farm Management Pocketbook's labour tables, rounded). The labour model reads it as demand.
- **Sales** (`sellEggs()`, `slaughter()`, `priceOf()`, `SALE_PRICE`): eggs at about £3 a kg (about £2 a dozen) and carcass at AHDB's rough farm-gate prices (lamb £8, beef £5.50, pork and mutton £3). Eggs leave the node's stock to `sold` and the money comes into the purse (`PURSE`, the kitchen's). A slaughter takes some head (all by default) off the herd, moves their share of the node's `liveweight` out (the carcass to `sold`, offal and hide to `decay`) and pays for whole carcasses, `head × weight × dressing × price`. The graph only holds the gain made on the node, so the £ are for the whole animals and the kg that move are that share of the stock. Both keep the graph balanced.
- **Illness as an event** (`outbreak(node, herd, hours, days)`, `outbreaksOf(node)`, the `events` lever): when a herd falls ill the `livestock` system adds a `GameEvent` (kind `disease`, label `outbreak`, its product, `homeLevel` from `Herd.level` or the species' default, hens 1 and the rest 3; size 0.5, the half of what it makes that illness takes; 21 days, or from the treatment for 4 more days, `treatedOutbreak(event, hours)`, so the event's kg match the shorter illness) to the node's `events` lever and drops the ones that have ended. `showEvent()` shows it as the tile one level up ("Output −50 % for 21 days") and a regional tint two up ("outbreak: eggs −3 %") with the same kg lost at each; the herd's own loss over the illness is that half and a little more, as welfare falls behind it (the slow cost the event doesn't count). The event uses no dice of its own.
- **A herd of many** (`perHead(species, conditions, welfare?, manage?)`, `aggregate(perHead, head, days)`): one head's day (eggs, gain, meat, feed, grass, water, manure and its nitrogen, methane, manure gases and hours) from a herd of one at the good density, fed to need, so a region's flock or the nation's hens are the head times that, not a stepped herd each. It is the average of a big herd, not any one farm's, and equals `step()` on a herd of that many at the same density.

## Wiring

**The garden's hens (the playable garden, round two), as built:** bought in the shed (`hens`, £240), the `hens` node (kind `hens`, `src/data/garden.ts`'s `SITES`) goes on the lawn with its 6.8 m² moved from the lawn's grass, its `feed` and `water` stocks and a `herd` lever of three hens on 12 m² (the run and time out on the lawn), `disease: false` until part 12's vet; ways to the tap, the kitchen, the heap and the air. The `livestock` system runs on the day tick after the shed and before the crops. The gardener's daily `hens` job keeps them (`docs/systems/gardener.md`); the eggs go to the kitchen and are eaten against the dairy and eggs group (`docs/systems/kitchen.md`); the droppings are cleared to the heap each Saturday, with their carbon and nitrogen. Feed is bought from the purse as it's used, so a purse that can't pay leaves them short: welfare and laying fall.


- **Nodes and stocks** (`LIVE`): a node per enclosure (a hen house, a field, a paddock) with `feed` (kgFeed, product `feed`), `water` (L), and, made by the flows, `manure` (kgWaste, product `greens`), `eggs` (kgFood, `eggs`), `liveweight` (kgFood, `liveweight`) and `nitrogen.organic` (kgN). A field the herd grazes also has the soil's stocks (`SOIL`), and `Herd.field` names the soil node if it isn't the herd's own.
- **Edges to add in `data/garden.ts` or the level's data:** herd node ↔ `heap` carrying `kgWaste`, `kgCO2e`, `kgN`; the air ↔ the field's soil node carrying `kgCO2e` (the field node's own edge to the air, if it has one); herd node ↔ the air carrying `kgCO2e`. (`graph.ts` needs nothing new: feed leaves to `eaten`, water to `evapotranspiration`, eggs, growth and manure nitrogen arrive from `growth`, droppings and gases from `decay`. A `feeding` boundary would read better than borrowing `eaten` and `growth`; the wiring part may add one.)
- **System:** add `livestock` (exported, `day` tick) to `systems.ts` after `weather` and `carbon`, before the kitchen (eggs must be laid before the day's collection). A `herd` lever is set with `setHerd(node, newHerd('hen', 3, 12, {disease: false}))`; a command sets `head`, `area`, `manage`, `shelter` and treats (`treat()`).
- **Jobs:** the gardener feeds and waters (into `feed` and `water`), collects `eggs` to the kitchen and calls `clearOut(ctx, node)` to move droppings to the heap. Carbon's `heapDay` will compost manure at its green-waste factors.
- **Map:** hens and sheep on their ground from `head`; the ground's colour from `ground`; eggs and droppings as items carried; a sick herd from `ill`; the air dial reads `enteric methane` and `manure methane and nitrous oxide` flows.
- **Panel and Explain:** welfare, `feedMet`/`waterMet`, `risk`, `stocking()`, and `PER_KG` for the diet question; each figure has its source in the model's header.
