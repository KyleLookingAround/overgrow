# Livestock

A herd or flock kept on a node: what it eats, drinks and makes, the manure and methane it gives off, its welfare and the chance it falls ill (`src/sim/models/livestock.ts`, with its plausibility test in `src/sim/models/livestock.test.ts`), from the species' figures in `src/data/livestock.ts`. Written ahead of the parts that wire it: part 6 (the garden's three hens) and part 12 (the smallholding's flock and pigs or sheep). Nothing is registered in `systems.ts` yet, so nothing ships.

- **Species** (`SPECIES`): laying hens, ewes, lambs, pigs (growers) and cattle, each with intake (kg dry matter a head a day), gain or lay rate, water, the temperatures it's comfortable between, space per head, how crowding hurts it, its enteric methane (IPCC Tier 1), nitrogen excreted (IPCC table 10.19) and manure. Poore & Nemecek's land, water and emissions per kg of product (`PER_KG`) are the diet question's numbers, for the Explain card. A ewe's lambs and a cow's milk are data only until part 12.
- **A day** (`step()`, pure, no graph): a herd (`Herd`, kept on its node as the `herd` lever, replaced each day) takes what grass its sward gives, then the node's `feed`, and drinks from its `water`. Hens lay `layRate` × the light (a floor of 0.4 at 8 hours, full from 13) × welfare's share × the cold's share × the feed and water met (a missed feed or an empty trough cuts today's eggs); growers gain up to finishing weight. Three hens on natural light lay about 265 eggs a year each, about a third as many a day in December as in June.
- **Manure and gases:** manure follows what was eaten: fresh mass, carbon (kg CO₂e of carbon) and nitrogen (IPCC's kg N per 1,000 kg a day). Enteric methane is IPCC's Tier 1 kg a head a year (sheep 8, cattle 57, pigs 1.5, poultry 0) at 27 times CO₂ (AR6). Manure is managed three ways: `heap` (droppings wait on the node until `clearOut()` moves them, with their carbon and nitrogen, to the heap; the heap's own gases are the carbon model's, on the manure's mass, so none are counted here), `spread` (methane conversion 0.1 %, nitrous oxide 1 % of the nitrogen, EF1) and `pasture` (1 %, and the species' EF3: 2 % for cattle, pigs and poultry, 1 % for sheep), the last two straight into the field's fresh carbon and organic nitrogen, with a tenth to a sixth of the nitrogen lost to the air. `availableN()` is RB209's share a crop can use in the year (poultry 30 %, farmyard manure 10 %).
- **Welfare** (0–1, slow): heads toward space (crowding above the good density, and the ground's condition) × feed met × water met × warmth, falling over about 12 days and mending over 30. Below 0.8 it cuts eggs and gain, to nothing at 0.2. Overstocking also wears the ground bare (pigs fastest) and a sward grazed to the roots wears it further, over a season; grass grows to 0.012 kg/m² a day at 15 °C and above, scaled by the ground's condition.
- **Illness:** `diseaseRisk()` is the chance in a step (a species' yearly chance, up with density and down welfare), kept on the herd as `risk`; the system rolls it once a day from its own `Rng` (`fallsIll()`), and a sick herd makes half as much for 21 days, or 4 once `treat()`ed (part 12's vet). A herd with `disease: false` never falls ill.
- **Per kg** (`footprint()`): a lamb's methane and manure gases come to about 15 kg CO₂e a kg of meat (with its ewe's share), a third of Poore & Nemecek's 40 for the whole chain; a pig's about 0.6, an egg's about 0.2.
- **Speed:** the `day` tick only. Measured headless in Node 22 on the session's machine: about 3 µs a herd, and 0.03 ms a game day for twelve herds (hens and ewes, flows applied), so part 6 and part 12's few herds take a few per cent of the parts-5-to-14 reserve's 1.0 ms.

## Wiring

- **Nodes and stocks** (`LIVE`): a node per enclosure (a hen house, a field, a paddock) with `feed` (kgFeed, product `feed`), `water` (L), and, made by the flows, `manure` (kgWaste, product `greens`), `eggs` (kgFood, `eggs`), `liveweight` (kgFood, `liveweight`) and `nitrogen.organic` (kgN). A field the herd grazes also has the soil's stocks (`SOIL`), and `Herd.field` names the soil node if it isn't the herd's own.
- **Edges to add in `data/garden.ts` or the level's data:** herd node ↔ `heap` carrying `kgWaste`, `kgCO2e`, `kgN`; herd node ↔ its field node carrying `kgCO2e`, `kgN`; herd node ↔ the air carrying `kgCO2e`. (`graph.ts` needs nothing new: feed leaves to `eaten`, water to `evapotranspiration`, eggs, growth and manure nitrogen arrive from `growth`, droppings and gases from `decay`. A `feeding` boundary would read better than borrowing `eaten` and `growth`; the wiring part may add one.)
- **System:** add `livestock` (exported, `day` tick) to `systems.ts` after `weather` and `carbon`, before the kitchen (eggs must be laid before the day's collection). A `herd` lever is set with `setHerd(node, newHerd('hen', 3, 12, {disease: false}))`; a command sets `head`, `area`, `manage`, `shelter` and treats (`treat()`).
- **Jobs:** the gardener feeds and waters (into `feed` and `water`), collects `eggs` to the kitchen and calls `clearOut(ctx, node)` to move droppings to the heap. Carbon's `heapDay` will compost manure at its green-waste factors.
- **Map:** hens and sheep on their ground from `head`; the ground's colour from `ground`; eggs and droppings as items carried; a sick herd from `ill`; the air dial reads `enteric methane` and `manure methane and nitrous oxide` flows.
- **Panel and Explain:** welfare, `feedMet`/`waterMet`, `risk`, `stocking()`, and `PER_KG` for the diet question; each figure has its source in the model's header.
